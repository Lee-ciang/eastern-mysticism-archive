# Overview

The Earthly Branches are a set of twelve signs used in traditional Chinese timekeeping and symbolic classification.

They are known as Di Zhi.

The branches are closely linked with the Twelve Zodiac Animals.

This makes them one of the most important structural systems behind the Chinese Zodiac.

# Twelve Branches

The twelve branches can mark years, months, hours, directions, and symbolic positions in traditional systems.

In popular zodiac culture, each branch is paired with an animal.

This connects the branches to `twelve-zodiac-animals`.

# Branches and Animals

The animal cycle is easier to remember than the branch names.

However, the branch system is the older structural frame behind the animals.

`zodiac-years` explains how the animal cycle is applied to years.

# Stems and Branches

The Earthly Branches combine with the `heavenly-stems`.

Together they form the `sexagenary-cycle`.

This sixty-part cycle is central to traditional calendrical naming.

# Symbolic Associations

Branches may be associated with directions, months, times of day, animals, and other symbolic correspondences.

These associations can overlap with `bagua`, `yin-yang`, and `five-elements`.

They show how calendar systems can become symbolic maps.
