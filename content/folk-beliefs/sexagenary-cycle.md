# Overview

The Sexagenary Cycle is a sixty-part cycle formed by combining the Ten Heavenly Stems with the Twelve Earthly Branches.

It is known as Gan Zhi.

The cycle is one of the major structures behind traditional Chinese timekeeping.

It strongly connects `heavenly-stems`, `earthly-branches`, and `zodiac-years`.

# Sixty Combinations

The cycle works by pairing one stem with one branch.

Because there are ten stems and twelve branches, the paired sequence repeats after sixty combinations.

This creates the familiar sixty-year cycle used in cultural references to birth years and calendrical time.

# Relationship to Stems

`heavenly-stems` provides the ten-part half of the system.

The stems are often associated with Yin-Yang and the Five Elements.

This gives the cycle a cosmological layer beyond simple numbering.

# Relationship to Branches

`earthly-branches` provides the twelve-part half of the system.

The branches are paired with the zodiac animals.

This is why the Sexagenary Cycle is closely connected with the Chinese Zodiac.

# Zodiac Years

`zodiac-years` explains how animal years are named and remembered.

The Sexagenary Cycle adds more detail by combining animal branches with stems.

A year may therefore be understood through both animal and elemental-symbolic associations.

# Five Elements

The stems are often linked with the Five Elements.

# Yin-Yang Context

Stem and branch systems are also interpreted through Yin and Yang alternation.

This connects the cycle to `yin-yang`.

The archive presents this as symbolic classification, not as a predictive guarantee.

# Calendar and Festival Context

The cycle belongs to traditional timekeeping.

It relates to `lunar-calendar` and can appear around almanacs, festivals, birthdays, and cultural age cycles.
