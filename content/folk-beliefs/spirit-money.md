# Overview

Spirit money, often known in English as joss paper, is a ritual paper offering used in Chinese folk religion, ancestor veneration, funerary rites, and festival observances. It is commonly burned as a symbolic gift for ancestors, deities, or spirits.

The practice is part of a larger offering system. This archive treats spirit money as a cultural and ritual object, not as proof of supernatural transfer.

# Historical Context

Paper offerings developed over time within Chinese funerary and religious traditions. Earlier burial goods and material offerings gradually influenced symbolic paper substitutes. These allowed families to present goods ritually without placing actual valuables in graves.

Spirit money became common in ancestor rites, funerals, temple observances, Ghost Month practices, and Qingming offerings.

# Forms and Materials

Spirit money may appear as gold or silver paper, printed currency-like sheets, folded ingots, or paper goods representing objects. In modern contexts, paper offerings may also represent houses, clothing, vehicles, or other symbolic items.

The forms vary by region, ritual context, and family custom.

# Ancestor Veneration

In ancestor veneration, spirit money may be burned as an offering to deceased family members. It is often presented alongside incense, food, tea, flowers, or other ritual goods.

The act expresses remembrance, care, and continuing obligation toward previous generations.

# Funeral and Festival Use

Spirit money is common in funeral offerings and during Qingming or Ghost Month. At funerals, it may accompany rites for the newly deceased. During Qingming, it may be offered at graves. During Ghost Month, it may be offered for hungry ghosts or wandering spirits.

These uses show how one ritual object can move across family, festival, and broader spirit contexts.

# Symbolic Meaning

Spirit money symbolizes exchange, care, and continuity between the living and the dead. Burning transforms the paper offering, making the ritual action visible through flame, smoke, and ash.

The meaning is symbolic and religious, not economic in a literal modern sense.

# Cultural Significance

Spirit money is an important cluster node because it connects ancestor veneration, funeral offerings, Ghost Month, Qingming, incense offering, and household ritual.
