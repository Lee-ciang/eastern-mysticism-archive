# Overview

The Dragon Boat Festival, also known as Duanwu, is a traditional festival associated with summer, boat races, rice dumplings, protective customs, commemoration, and seasonal danger. It is one of the major festivals in the Chinese lunar calendar and belongs to a wider yearly pattern of renewal, remembrance, offering, and protection.

In this archive, the Dragon Boat Festival is treated as part of Chinese folk belief and seasonal festival culture. Its practices vary by region, community, and historical layer.

# Place in the Festival Cycle

Within the Festival & Calendar Cluster, `dragon-boat-festival` links to `traditional-festival-cycle` and `lunar-calendar` as an early summer observance. It appears after `qingming-festival` and before `ghost-festival`, `ghost-month`, `mid-autumn-festival`, and `winter-solstice-customs`.

This position matters because summer was often associated with heat, illness, insects, miasma, and the need for protection. Festival customs could therefore express both celebration and caution.

# Protective Customs

Protective practices are an important part of Dragon Boat Festival culture. Some customs involve aromatic herbs, charms, colored threads, images, protective inscriptions, or ritual gestures intended to ward off harmful influences. These themes connect the festival to `protective-folk-customs`, `household-protection`, and `warding-practices`.

The archive treats protective meanings as cultural and symbolic traditions, not as practical guarantees.

# Dragon Symbolism

The dragon boat itself links the festival to dragon imagery. The page `dragon-symbol` provides a symbolic bridge into the archive's Symbols section. Dragons can be associated with water, power, rain, vitality, and imperial or auspicious meaning depending on context.

Dragon boat racing may be public, athletic, communal, commemorative, and ritual at the same time. Its meaning depends on local tradition.

# Offerings and Commemoration

Food and offerings may appear in Dragon Boat Festival customs. Zongzi, rice dumplings, are especially well known. In some traditions, the festival is also associated with remembrance of Qu Yuan, though the festival has multiple historical and regional layers.

`festival-offerings`, `spirit-offerings`, and `temple-offerings` help place festival foods and ritual goods within a wider language of offering and commemoration.

# Ancestors and Seasonal Memory

Dragon Boat Festival is not primarily an ancestor festival in the same way as `qingming-festival`, but it still belongs to a culture where seasonal observance and family continuity overlap. Families may gather, prepare food, visit temples, or maintain inherited customs.

This connects the page to `ancestor-veneration` and `ancestor-spirits` as part of the broader festival network.

# Relationship to Other Festivals

`chinese-new-year-customs` emphasizes renewal, `qingming-festival` emphasizes remembrance, Dragon Boat Festival emphasizes summer protection and communal vitality, `ghost-festival` addresses spirits, `mid-autumn-festival` centers reunion and the moon, and `winter-solstice-customs` marks seasonal return.

Together they form a cycle of changing ritual concerns across the year.

# Cultural Context

Modern Dragon Boat Festival observance may include races, food, public events, family meals, temple customs, protective decorations, and heritage celebrations. In diaspora settings, dragon boat racing can become a major public expression of cultural identity.
