# Overview

The Ghost Festival is a traditional observance associated with spirits, offerings, ritual caution, and the seventh lunar month. It is closely linked to `ghost-month`, but the festival itself often refers to a focal ritual date within that broader period. Customs may include food offerings, incense, paper goods, communal rites, temple ceremonies, and acts of remembrance.

This archive treats the Ghost Festival as a cultural and religious tradition. Practices vary widely, and the meanings may be Buddhist, Taoist, folk religious, ancestral, communal, or family-centered depending on context.

# Ghost Festival and Ghost Month

The Ghost Festival belongs inside the wider `ghost-month` cycle. Ghost Month traditions may describe spirit gates opening, wandering spirits becoming more active, and families or communities making offerings to maintain respect and harmony.

Related pages such as `ghost-month-and-ancestors`, `ghost-gates`, and `wandering-spirits` explain the surrounding belief network. The festival is therefore not isolated. It is part of a ritual season.

# Calendar Placement

The `lunar-calendar` gives the Ghost Festival its place in the traditional year. Within the `traditional-festival-cycle`, it sits after spring and summer festivals and before autumn moon observances. This placement matters because the festival year moves through renewal, remembrance, protection, offering, and reunion.

The Ghost Festival has a different tone from `chinese-new-year-customs`, `qingming-festival`, `dragon-boat-festival`, `mid-autumn-festival`, and `winter-solstice-customs`, but it belongs to the same larger rhythm of ritual time.

# Ancestors and Wandering Spirits

The Ghost Festival can involve both ancestors and non-ancestral spirits. Some offerings may be directed toward family dead, while others may be made for wandering, neglected, or hungry spirits. This distinction connects the page to `ancestor-veneration`, `ancestor-spirits`, `spirit-offerings`, and `spirit-money`.

Ancestor rites emphasize kinship and remembrance. Offerings to wandering spirits often express compassion, caution, or communal responsibility toward the unseen.

# Offerings and Temple Practice

Offerings are central to many Ghost Festival customs. Food, incense, paper money, candles, and ritual tables may be prepared. `festival-offerings`, `spirit-offerings`, and `temple-offerings` explain how offerings operate across household, temple, and community settings.

Some communities hold public rituals, performances, or temple ceremonies. These may be interpreted as ways to comfort spirits, transfer merit, protect the living, or maintain cosmic and social balance.

# Relationship to Afterlife Beliefs

# Cultural Context

In modern communities, the Ghost Festival may appear as temple ritual, household offering, public performance, family custom, or cultural heritage. Some people observe it religiously; others recognize it through stories, taboos, foods, incense, and community events.
