# Overview

The Chinese Zodiac is a cultural system that associates years with a cycle of twelve animals.

It is known in Chinese as Sheng Xiao.

The system belongs to calendar culture, folk belief, symbolic classification, and traditional astrology.

In this archive, the Chinese Zodiac is treated as a historical and cultural system.

It is not presented as a factual method for predicting fate.

# Calendar Context

The Chinese Zodiac is closely connected with the lunar calendar.

It is often discussed during New Year customs, family conversations, almanacs, and festival cycles.

The page `lunar-calendar` explains the wider timing system behind many traditional observances.

The page `traditional-festival-cycle` shows how calendar ideas shape yearly ritual culture.

# Twelve Animals

The most familiar part of the system is the sequence of twelve animals.

These are discussed in `twelve-zodiac-animals`.

Each animal can carry symbolic associations, stories, personality images, and cultural meanings.

The animal cycle is especially visible in popular New Year imagery.

# Branches and Stems

The Zodiac is not only an animal list.

It is connected with the `earthly-branches` and the `heavenly-stems`.

Together, stems and branches form the `sexagenary-cycle`.

That cycle creates a sixty-part system used for years, days, and other traditional time calculations.

# Zodiac Years

`zodiac-years` explains how years are associated with animals.

This topic should be understood as calendar symbolism.

It should not be treated as a modern predictive tool.

# Compatibility and Folklore

`zodiac-compatibility` discusses traditional ideas about harmony and difference among signs.

Such ideas appear in folklore, marriage talk, family custom, and popular culture.

The archive presents them as cultural beliefs rather than reliable rules.

# Elements and Cosmology

The Zodiac is often combined with the Five Elements.

`five-elements-and-zodiac` explains this relationship.

Related systems include `five-elements`, `yin-yang`, `qi-energy`, and `cosmology-in-taoism`.

# Divination and Symbolism

The Zodiac sometimes appears near divination and almanac traditions.

It is therefore linked with `i-ching-divination`, `bagua`, and `heaven-earth-human`.

These systems are not identical.

They share a broader cultural interest in patterned time and symbolic order.
