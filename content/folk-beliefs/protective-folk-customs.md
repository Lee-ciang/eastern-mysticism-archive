# Overview

Protective folk customs are everyday practices used in Chinese folk religion and household culture to symbolically guard people, homes, children, thresholds, kitchens, ancestors, or seasonal transitions. They may include door images, red paper, offerings, taboos, charms, cleaning rituals, incense, or protective sayings.

The archive treats these customs as cultural and historical traditions. It does not present them as guaranteed protection.

# Historical Context

Protective customs developed through local practice, family tradition, folk religion, Taoist influence, festival cycles, and regional belief. Many were transmitted through households rather than formal institutions.

They often respond to moments of vulnerability: birth, illness, death, moving house, New Year, Ghost Month, travel, or changes in family fortune.

# Household Protection

Many protective folk customs focus on the home. Door gods may be posted, kitchens may receive offerings, thresholds may be cleaned, and altars may be maintained.

These practices show how domestic space is treated as ritually meaningful.

# Seasonal Renewal

New Year customs often include cleaning, replacing door images, posting red paper, offering incense, and preparing the household for a new cycle. Protective meaning is combined with renewal, auspiciousness, and family continuity.

Ghost Month may involve additional caution, offerings, or taboos in some communities.

# Ancestors and Protection

Ancestor ritual can also be part of protective folk culture. Remembered ancestors may be understood within tradition as part of the family's moral and ritual order.

Offerings and household altar care express continuity and respect.

# Symbols and Charms

Protective charms, talismans, guardian symbols, and evil-aversion images give visual form to protective concerns. They are often placed where boundaries or vulnerability are emphasized.

These objects make belief visible in everyday life.

# Cultural Significance

Protective folk customs are the everyday side of the Protective Practices Cluster. They link household protection, door gods, Kitchen God customs, ancestor ritual, warding practices, purification, and protective symbolism.
