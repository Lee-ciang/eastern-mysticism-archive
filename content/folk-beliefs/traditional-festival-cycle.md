# Overview

The traditional festival cycle refers to the recurring pattern of seasonal observances, household rites, temple offerings, ancestor customs, and lunar-calendar festivals in Chinese cultural life. It is not one single holiday, but a network of ritual moments spread across the year. These observances connect family memory, agricultural time, local temples, household protection, spirit beliefs, and ideas of renewal.

# Lunar Time and Cultural Rhythm

The `lunar-calendar` is the organizing framework behind many traditional festival dates. Months, full moons, seasonal nodes, and ritual periods give cultural meaning to time. Festivals often mark transition points: the start of the year, the return of spring, the remembrance of ancestors, the danger of summer heat, the opening of ghostly gates, the autumn moon, and the winter solstice.

This makes festival time different from ordinary calendar time. A date can become ritually active because it gathers family duties, offerings, taboos, memories, and symbolic meanings.

# Ancestors and Family Continuity

Ancestor-related observances are central to the festival cycle. `qingming-festival` is one of the clearest examples because it joins tomb sweeping, family remembrance, offerings, and filial piety. The pages `ancestor-veneration`, `ancestor-spirits`, `ancestor-ritual`, `household-altar`, and `spirit-offerings` show how festival practice often keeps the living and the dead in relationship.

The festival cycle therefore does not only celebrate seasonal change. It also renews kinship across generations.

# Spirits, Ghost Month, and Festival Offerings

Some festivals address unsettled or wandering spirits. `ghost-festival` and `ghost-month` belong to this side of the cluster. They connect ritual caution, offerings, spirit money, ancestor remembrance, wandering spirits, and ideas about unseen worlds.

`festival-offerings` is a practical bridge page for this cluster because many festivals include food, incense, paper goods, lamps, fruit, tea, wine, or symbolic gifts. Offerings may be made at home, graves, community altars, or temples. `temple-offerings` and `spirit-offerings` explain the broader ritual language behind these acts.

# Seasonal and Temple Context

The festival cycle is also connected to temples and communal observance. Temple festivals may honor deities, renew local bonds, mark seasonal transitions, or provide a place for collective offerings. `seasonal-rituals` helps connect household custom with public ritual time.

Festivals such as `dragon-boat-festival`, `mid-autumn-festival`, and `winter-solstice-customs` show how seasonal rhythm becomes cultural expression. Food, stories, protective customs, moon symbolism, family meals, and ritual offerings all help make time meaningful.

# Cultural Context

The traditional festival cycle is one of the strongest ways Chinese folk belief appears in everyday life. It turns the year into a sequence of remembered duties, protective gestures, family gatherings, temple visits, and symbolic renewals.

This archive treats these practices as cultural and historical traditions. Local customs vary widely by region, lineage, temple, migration history, and family habit. The same festival may be interpreted as religious, familial, seasonal, civic, or cultural depending on context.
