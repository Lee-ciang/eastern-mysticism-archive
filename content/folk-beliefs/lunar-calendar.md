# Overview

The lunar calendar is the timekeeping framework behind many Chinese festivals, household rites, temple observances, and seasonal customs. It organizes months according to lunar cycles while also relating the year to seasonal change. In cultural practice, it helps determine when families prepare offerings, visit graves, honor ancestors, observe taboos, celebrate reunion, and mark transitions in the year.

In this archive, the lunar calendar is treated as a cultural and ritual framework. It is not simply a date system. It is a way of giving time symbolic weight.

# Ancestors and Lunar Time

Ancestor-related practices often depend on ritual dates. `qingming-festival`, `ancestor-veneration`, `ancestor-spirits`, and `spirit-offerings` show how time can structure relationships between living families and the dead. A festival date may call people to remember, clean tombs, prepare food, burn incense, or make offerings.

The calendar therefore functions as a memory system. It helps families return to obligations that might otherwise fade in ordinary daily life.

# Spirits, Ghost Month, and Taboos

The lunar calendar also organizes spirit-related periods. `ghost-month` and `ghost-festival` are associated with the seventh lunar month, when traditions may describe spirits as more active. Offerings, cautionary customs, and communal rituals become especially important.

These ideas vary widely, but the calendar gives them a shared cultural location. A ritual period becomes recognizable because it returns each year.

# Offerings and Temple Observance

Festival time often includes offerings. `festival-offerings`, `spirit-offerings`, and `temple-offerings` explain how food, incense, paper goods, fruit, tea, candles, and symbolic items may be used in different settings. The same calendar date can bring activity to household altars, ancestral graves, local temples, and community spaces.

Temple observances may also follow deity birthdays, seasonal nodes, and local ritual calendars. This links calendar culture to temple life, not only family life.

# Cosmological Context

Traditional calendar thinking also intersects with wider Chinese cosmology. Pages such as `heaven-and-earth`, `cosmic-order`, `yin-yang`, and `five-elements` help explain why time was often understood as patterned, seasonal, and relational. Calendar dates were not merely numerical. They could express phases of growth, decline, danger, renewal, or balance.

# Cultural Context

In modern life, many people encounter the lunar calendar through festival dates, zodiac language, family customs, temple announcements, food traditions, and public holidays. Some uses are religious, some familial, and some cultural.

The archive presents lunar calendar customs as historically layered practices that can vary by region, dialect group, diaspora community, and family tradition.
