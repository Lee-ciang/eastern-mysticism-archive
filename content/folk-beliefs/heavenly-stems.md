# Overview

The Heavenly Stems are a set of ten signs used in traditional Chinese timekeeping and symbolic classification.

They are known as Tian Gan.

Together with the Twelve Earthly Branches, they form the `sexagenary-cycle`.

This page treats the stems as part of historical calendar culture and traditional symbolism.

# Ten Stems

The ten stems are often discussed in relation to Yin and Yang and the Five Elements.

They are not animal signs.

They are a separate symbolic sequence that works with the branches.

# Stems and Branches

The Heavenly Stems pair with the `earthly-branches`.

This pairing creates a cycle of sixty combinations.

That system is explained in `sexagenary-cycle`.

# Calendar Role

The stems have been used to mark years, days, and other time units in traditional systems.

Their use belongs to a broader Chinese calendar environment.

The page `lunar-calendar` provides important background.

# Relationship to Zodiac Animals

The Chinese Zodiac animals are associated with the Earthly Branches.

However, zodiac years are often described through both stems and branches.

This connects the stems to `zodiac-years` and `twelve-zodiac-animals`.
