# Overview

Ghost gates are a folk religious image associated especially with Ghost Month. In many Chinese traditions, the seventh lunar month is described as a period when gates between the underworld or spirit realms and the human world open. Spirits may move more freely, and communities respond with offerings, rituals, taboos, and protective customs.

This archive treats ghost gates as a cultural and ritual concept. The phrase does not refer to a physical gate that can be verified in ordinary terms. It expresses a symbolic opening of boundaries between the living and the dead, between household order and unsettled spirits, and between ordinary time and festival time.

# Relation to Ghost Month

Ghost Month gives ghost-gate imagery its strongest context. The opening of the gates may be linked to wandering spirits, hungry ghosts, or souls temporarily released from underworld realms. Public offerings may be prepared for spirits without descendants or stable ritual support.

Families may also continue ancestor-related observances, though Ghost Month is not identical to ordinary ancestor veneration. The distinction matters: ancestor rites usually focus on named family dead, while Ghost Month often extends ritual attention to anonymous or wandering spirits.

# Boundaries and Ritual Space

Ghost gates express the idea that boundaries can become temporarily permeable. This makes thresholds, doors, streets, temples, water edges, crossroads, and outdoor offering spaces symbolically important. Some customs place offerings outside the home to avoid inviting wandering spirits into domestic space.

This boundary logic links ghost gates to threshold rituals and protective practices. Taboos, offerings, incense, talismans, and community rites can all serve as ways of managing the perceived risks of an open spirit-world boundary.

# Underworld and Spirit Realms

Ghost gates connect folk belief to underworld traditions. In some narratives, spirits emerge from underworld courts or realms overseen by judges such as the Ten Kings of Hell. In others, the image is looser and primarily seasonal.

The key point is that ghost gates place the human world within a larger ritual geography. The living are not imagined as isolated from the dead; instead, seasonal time can bring contact, obligation, and caution.
