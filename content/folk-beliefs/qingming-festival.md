# Overview

Qingming Festival, often translated as Tomb-Sweeping Day, is a major Chinese seasonal observance associated with ancestor remembrance, grave care, family gatherings, and offerings to the deceased.

It is one of the most important public expressions of ancestor veneration. The festival connects family memory, seasonal renewal, filial piety, and ritual care for ancestral graves.

# Historical Context

Qingming has roots in seasonal calendars, ancestral rites, and historical festival traditions. Over time, it became strongly associated with visiting graves, maintaining burial sites, and honoring ancestors.

The date usually falls in early April according to the solar calendar. It belongs to a seasonal moment associated with spring clarity, renewal, and attention to the landscape.

# Tomb Sweeping

One of the best-known Qingming practices is tomb sweeping. Families may clean graves, remove weeds, repair markers, and arrange offerings. These actions show respect for ancestors and maintain the physical site of family memory. Where burial sites are distant or inaccessible, families may use a columbarium, memorial hall, temple service, household remembrance, or another locally accepted form.

The practice is both ritual and practical. Caring for a grave is a visible expression of ongoing relationship.

# Offerings

Qingming offerings may include incense, food, tea, wine, fruit, flowers, candles, [spirit money](/folk-beliefs/spirit-money), or paper goods. Practices vary by region, family tradition, cemetery rules, migration history, and modern circumstances.

Offerings are traditionally understood as acts of respect and remembrance. The archive describes them as cultural and ritual practices, not as factual claims about the dead receiving material goods. Paper offerings are not universal, and this page provides no burning instructions.

# Filial Piety

Qingming is closely connected with [filial piety](/folk-beliefs/filial-piety-and-ancestors). It gives descendants a formal occasion to remember previous generations and acknowledge family continuity.

The festival often reinforces the idea that family obligation does not end with death.

Qingming is highly visible, but it is not the only occasion for [ancestor veneration](/folk-beliefs/ancestor-veneration). Death anniversaries, household dates, funeral memorial intervals, Lunar New Year, the winter solstice, Ghost Month, and local calendars can also structure remembrance.

# Relationship to Ghost Month

Qingming and [Ghost Month](/folk-beliefs/ghost-month) both involve the dead, but their emphasis differs. Qingming focuses strongly on family ancestors, burial sites, and spring grave care. Ghost Month is more broadly associated with wandering or unprovided-for spirits as well as some household ancestor observances.

Both festivals belong to the archive's larger afterlife and ancestor cluster.

# Modern Practice

In modern settings, Qingming may be observed through grave visits, temple rites, digital memorials, family meals, charitable acts, or simplified offerings. Migration, urban life, cemetery policy, environmental regulation, and work schedules have changed practice, but the festival remains culturally significant.
