# Overview

Ghost Month is a traditional festival period associated in many Chinese communities with wandering spirits, ancestors, offerings, public rites, and ritual caution. It is commonly linked to the seventh month of the lunar calendar, although calendars, names, observances, and intensity vary by region and religious tradition.

# Cultural Background

In some traditions, participants believe the boundary between the living and the dead becomes especially significant during this period. Buddhist narratives, Taoist ritual, popular religion, local festivals, household custom, and community performance have all shaped observance.

The period is broader than ancestor remembrance. [Ghost Month and Ancestors](/folk-beliefs/ghost-month-and-ancestors) explains how named family ancestors may receive household attention while public rites address [wandering spirits](/folk-beliefs/wandering-spirits) or unprovided-for dead outside the lineage.

# Taboos and Ritual Caution

Popular accounts may mention avoiding night swimming, whistling after dark, major life events, disturbance of roadside offerings, or disrespectful behavior around burial and ritual places. These customs are not universal rules. Their wording, seriousness, and observance differ among regions, generations, families, and media traditions.

The archive records taboos as cultural beliefs rather than verified dangers. Sensational lists can obscure the month's communal, charitable, memorial, artistic, and religious dimensions.

# Ritual Practices

Traditional practices may include food and [spirit offerings](/rituals/spirit-offerings), incense, ancestor remembrance, temple services, communal performances, lanterns, or charitable acts. Recipients and meanings depend on context: an offering to a family ancestor is not automatically the same as a public offering to unknown spirits.

Paper offerings vary historically and regionally. Modern observance is shaped by fire law, air-quality rules, designated facilities, environmental concerns, urban housing, and family preference. No hazardous burning guidance is provided here.

# Symbolic Meaning

Ghost Month reflects cultural concerns about memory, neglected death, ritual hospitality, social boundaries, compassion, and order between the living and the dead. These are participant beliefs and inherited ritual interpretations, not verified descriptions of supernatural events.

# Modern Interpretation

Today, Ghost Month may be understood as religious practice, folk tradition, community festival, family remembrance, seasonal caution, performance culture, or heritage. It remains distinct from [Qingming](/folk-beliefs/qingming-festival), which is more strongly centered on family graves and ancestral remembrance.
