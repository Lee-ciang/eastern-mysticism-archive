# Overview

Funeral offerings are ritual gifts presented during Chinese funerary practices to honor the deceased, support family mourning, and mark the transition from death toward ancestral remembrance. Offerings may include incense, food, tea, flowers, candles, paper goods, spirit money, clothing, or symbolic objects.

This archive describes funeral offerings as cultural and religious traditions. It does not present claims about the afterlife as factual certainty.

# Historical Context

Chinese funerary offerings have a long history, shaped by ancestor veneration, folk religion, Confucian family ethics, Buddhist influence, Taoist ritual, and local custom. Practices vary widely by region, family, religious affiliation, and historical period.

Offerings help structure the period of mourning and give descendants a way to express respect, grief, duty, and continuity.

# Ritual Function

Funeral offerings may be presented at wakes, funeral halls, gravesites, temples, or household altars. They can accompany prayers, chanting, bowing, processions, or memorial rites.

The offerings mark the deceased as someone still held within family memory and ritual attention.

# Spirit Money and Paper Goods

Spirit money is one of the most recognizable funeral offerings. Paper goods may also be burned as symbolic provisions. These actions express care for the deceased within the ritual imagination of the tradition.

The archive treats this as symbolic and cultural practice rather than a literal description of material transfer.

# Food and Incense

Food offerings may represent hospitality, nourishment, and family continuity. Incense offering marks respect and creates a ritual atmosphere. Together, these actions help transform ordinary mourning into structured ceremony.

Different families and communities follow different offering customs.

# Relationship to Ancestor Ritual

Funeral offerings are closely related to ancestor ritual because funerary rites help move the deceased into the family memory system. After funeral observances, later rituals may occur at household altars, graves, ancestral halls, or memorial days.

This transition is central to the ancestor cluster.

# Relationship to Qingming and Ghost Month

Funeral offerings differ from festival offerings, but they share materials and meanings with Qingming and Ghost Month. Incense, food, and spirit money may appear across all three contexts.

This overlap helps the archive connect funerary practice with broader afterlife and ancestor beliefs.
