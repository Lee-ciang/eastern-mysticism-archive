# Overview

Incense offering is one of the most widespread ritual actions in Chinese folk religion, Taoism, Buddhist-influenced temple culture, and household practice. It commonly involves lighting incense sticks and presenting them before deities, ancestors, spirits, or sacred images.

The practice is best understood as a cultural and religious form of respect, communication, remembrance, and ritual presence.

# Ritual Meaning

Incense smoke is often interpreted as a symbolic bridge between the human and spiritual realms. The rising smoke can represent sincerity, prayer, offering, purification, or the transmission of intention.

The archive treats these meanings as traditional religious interpretations rather than factual claims about supernatural communication.

# Household and Temple Contexts

In household settings, incense may be offered at ancestral tablets, deity images, or small domestic altars. In temples, incense offering often occurs at main halls, side altars, courtyards, or entrance areas.

The exact number of incense sticks, order of bowing, and placement of offerings varies by region, temple, family, and tradition.

# Connection to Ancestors

Incense is especially important in ancestor veneration. Offering incense can mark remembrance, respect, and continuity between living family members and the deceased.

It may be used during daily observances, memorial days, funerary periods, Qingming, Ghost Month, or household rites.

# Taoist and Folk Religious Use

In Taoist ritual contexts, incense may help establish sacred space, mark the beginning of a rite, honor deities, or accompany petitions and invocations.

In folk practice, incense is often part of a broader set of offerings including fruit, tea, food, flowers, candles, or paper goods.

# Cultural Significance

Incense offering is foundational because it appears across many categories of the archive: rituals, folk religion, temples, ancestors, deities, protective practices, and festivals.
