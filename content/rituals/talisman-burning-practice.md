# Overview

Talisman burning practice refers to the ritual burning of written or printed talismans in Taoist and Chinese folk religious contexts. The act may involve paper Fu, written signs, seals, incense, prayers, offerings, purification, or protective rites.

This page expands the existing `talisman-burning` topic by focusing on practice and context. It does not present ritual claims as factual outcomes.

# Burning as Transformation

Burning changes the talisman from paper into smoke and ash. In cultural interpretation, this transformation may symbolize transmission, release, purification, communication, or movement from visible to invisible form.
