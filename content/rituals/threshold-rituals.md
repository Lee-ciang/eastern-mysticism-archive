# Overview

Threshold rituals are practices focused on entrances, doorways, gates, windows, and other boundaries between inside and outside. In Chinese folk religion and household protection, thresholds are often treated as meaningful spaces where order, vulnerability, hospitality, and protection meet.

This archive describes threshold rituals as cultural and historical practices. It does not present protective claims as factual guarantees.

# Historical Context

Many Chinese household customs developed around doors and gates. Door gods, red paper inscriptions, talismans, incense, sweeping, offerings, and seasonal renewals all show how thresholds became ritual sites.

These practices were shaped by folk religion, Taoist symbolism, New Year customs, Feng Shui ideas, and local family traditions.

# Doorways as Boundaries

A doorway separates domestic space from the outside world. It allows entry, but also requires control. For this reason, doors often carry protective symbols.

Threshold rituals express a desire to maintain household order, welcome appropriate guests, and symbolically keep harmful influences outside.

# Common Ritual Forms

Threshold-related practices may include:

- posting door gods
- replacing New Year couplets
- placing talismans near doors
- lighting incense near an entrance
- sweeping or cleansing the doorway
- using Bagua-related objects
- making offerings to household protectors

The details vary greatly by region and family.

# Relationship to Feng Shui

Feng Shui often pays attention to entrances because they shape movement through a home or building. A doorway may be discussed in relation to qi flow, direction, visibility, and spatial arrangement.

This gives threshold rituals a bridge to Feng Shui interpretation.

# Relationship to Protection Ritual

Threshold rituals are a specialized form of protection ritual. They focus less on the whole household and more on the boundary where passage occurs.

They may overlap with warding practices, ritual purification, and protective talismans.

# Cultural Significance

Threshold rituals are important because they show how protection is often spatial. The home is not protected in the abstract; specific points such as doors, gates, altars, and kitchens become ritual centers.
