# Overview

Threshold talismans are protective or ritual signs placed near doors, gates, windows, or transitional spaces. In Chinese household and Taoist-related traditions, thresholds can be treated as vulnerable or powerful boundaries between inside and outside, familiar and unfamiliar, ordered and uncertain.

This archive treats threshold talismans as cultural symbols and ritual customs. It does not present them as objectively protective devices.

# Threshold as Boundary

A threshold is more than an architectural feature. It is a symbolic boundary. This is why threshold talismans connect closely to `door-protection-symbols`, `household-protection`, `warding-practices`, and `protection-ritual`.

Marking the threshold can express a desire for order, safety, blessing, or separation from harmful influence.

# Fu and Door Protection

Threshold talismans may use `fu-talismans`, `talisman-writing`, `talisman-paper`, `talisman-seals`, and `protective-fu-symbols`. Some are posted directly on doors; others may be placed nearby or used in a broader ritual.

They also relate to `household-talismans`, because threshold protection is one part of domestic ritual life.

# Ritual Purification and Warding

Threshold talismans may be used alongside `ritual-purification`, `protective-talismans`, and `protective-charms`. In cultural interpretation, they symbolically cleanse, guard, or seal a boundary.

The archive emphasizes that these meanings belong to religious and folk traditions rather than scientific claims.

# Burning and Replacement

Some threshold talismans may be replaced, removed, or burned after a period of use. `talisman-burning` and `talisman-burning-practice` explain how burning can transform ritual paper and mark renewal.
