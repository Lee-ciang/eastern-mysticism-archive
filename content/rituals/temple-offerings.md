# Overview

Temple offerings are ritual gifts presented in temples to deities, ancestors, spirits, guardians, or other sacred figures. Offerings may include incense, fruit, flowers, tea, wine, lamps, candles, food, paper goods, money donations, written petitions, or symbolic objects.

The archive treats temple offerings as cultural and religious practices. It does not present offerings as producing guaranteed supernatural results. Their meaning depends on context, recipient, tradition, and community interpretation.

# Types of Offerings

Incense is one of the most common offerings and may be made before entering a hall or standing before an altar. Food and fruit offerings can express respect, gratitude, hospitality, or fulfillment of a vow. Lamps and candles may symbolize clarity, continuity, or devotion.

Written petitions may be placed before deities or handled by ritual specialists. Paper offerings and spirit money are more common in ancestor, funerary, or spirit-related contexts than in every temple setting.

# Deities, Ancestors, and Spirits

Temple offerings can be directed to different recipients. Offerings to deities may express respect, thanks, or petition. Offerings connected to ancestor veneration emphasize memory and filial continuity. Offerings for spirits may relate to Ghost Month, funerals, or public rituals of care.

This distinction helps prevent random linking in the archive. Temple offerings are related to spirit offerings, but they are not identical. The temple setting gives them a public, architectural, and deity-centered dimension.

# Ritual Procedure

Offerings may be made at temple altars, courtyard burners, deity halls, or side shrines. Purification, bowing, incense, and spoken or silent intention may accompany the act. Temple customs vary widely and may be shaped by local management, religious lineage, safety concerns, and festival context.
