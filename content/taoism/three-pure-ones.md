# Overview

The Three Pure Ones, also known as the Sanqing, are among the highest divine figures in religious Taoism. They represent elevated cosmic and theological principles within Taoist deity systems.

They are especially important in temple iconography, ritual texts, and religious Taoist cosmology.

# Religious Context

The Three Pure Ones are not simply mythological characters. In Taoist religious traditions, they represent sacred levels of reality, celestial authority, and the refined expression of the Dao.

Different Taoist schools and ritual traditions may explain their roles in different ways.

# The Three Figures

The Three Pure Ones are commonly identified as:

- The Jade Pure One
- The Highest Pure One
- The Great Pure One

Their names are translated in several ways in English. The exact titles vary depending on source and transliteration.

# Symbolic Meaning

The Three Pure Ones can be understood as expressions of cosmic order and sacred hierarchy. They help organize Taoist theology in a way that connects the ineffable Dao with ritual, scripture, and divine administration.

They are often represented in temple images as dignified celestial figures.

# Ritual Significance

In religious Taoism, the Three Pure Ones may appear in liturgy, temple worship, invocations, and ritual settings. Their presence expresses the authority and sacred context of Taoist practice.

The archive treats these traditions as religious and cultural systems rather than making claims about supernatural reality.

# Relationship to Taoist Cosmology

The Three Pure Ones show how Taoism includes both philosophical ideas and elaborate religious structures. They link abstract concepts such as the Dao with devotional and ritual forms.
