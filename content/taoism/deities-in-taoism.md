# Overview

Deities in Taoism are divine, celestial, ancestral, and immortal figures found in religious Taoist traditions and related Chinese religious culture. They may appear in temples, ritual texts, talismans, liturgies, festivals, household worship, and popular devotional practice.

Taoist deity systems are diverse. They include high cosmic figures such as the Three Pure Ones, administrative figures such as the Jade Emperor, transformed sages such as Lord Lao, immortals, local gods, thunder deities, and figures shared with Chinese folk religion.

# Religious Context

Taoism includes philosophical writings about the Dao as well as religious systems with liturgy, priests, scriptures, temples, registers, and divine hierarchies. Deities belong especially to religious Taoism, though many figures also appear in folk religious practice.

The archive treats these deities as religious and cultural entities. It does not present theological claims as historical or scientific facts.

# Deities and the Dao

The Dao is not simply one deity among others. In Taoist thought, the Dao is the underlying way or process of reality. Deities may be understood as expressions, administrators, manifestations, or ritual figures within a cosmos ordered by the Dao.

This distinction matters because Taoism can include both abstract cosmology and richly populated religious worlds.

# Celestial Hierarchy

Many Taoist deity systems use hierarchical language. Celestial realms, divine offices, registers, and bureaucratic titles are common. This reflects the influence of Chinese administrative models on religious imagination.

The Jade Emperor, City God, Earth God, Kitchen God, and thunder deities all make sense within this broader pattern of sacred administration.

# Ritual Significance

Deities may be invoked in rituals, honored at altars, represented in temple images, or addressed through petitions and offerings. The specific ritual role depends on the deity, temple, lineage, and local tradition.

Taoist ritual does not treat deities as isolated personalities only. They often function within cosmic, moral, and bureaucratic systems.

# Relationship to Folk Religion

Many deities overlap with Chinese folk religion. Kitchen God, Earth God, City God, and Queen Mother of the West may appear in both Taoist and broader popular religious contexts.
