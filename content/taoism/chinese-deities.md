# Overview

Chinese deities are divine, ancestral, celestial, local, and symbolic figures found in Chinese religious culture.

They appear in Taoist temples, folk shrines, household altars, festivals, stories, ritual texts, and protective practices.

This page is the hub for the Chinese Deities Entity Cluster.

It treats deity traditions as cultural, historical, religious, and symbolic systems.

It does not present religious claims as factual proof of supernatural beings.

# Deity Systems

Chinese deity traditions are diverse.

Some figures belong closely to Taoist ritual systems.

Some belong to folk religion, local temple culture, or household worship.

Some appear in literature, festivals, lineage memory, and popular devotion.

# Cosmological Background

Chinese deity systems often reflect wider ideas of cosmic order.

Related concepts include `dao`, `qi-energy`, `yin-yang`, `five-elements`, `cosmology-in-taoism`, and `taoist-cosmology`.

The deities are part of a symbolic world where Heaven, Earth, humanity, ritual, and morality are linked.
