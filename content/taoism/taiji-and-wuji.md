# Overview

Taiji and Wuji are important concepts in Chinese cosmology and Taoist-influenced metaphysics. Wuji is often translated as the limitless, the undifferentiated, or the state before polarity. Taiji is often translated as the Supreme Ultimate and is associated with the emergence of Yin and Yang.

Together, Taiji and Wuji help explain how Taoist cosmology describes movement from undifferentiated potential into patterned reality.

This is the cluster's metaphysical sequence entry. It focuses on how non-polarity and polarity are related. The [Yin Yang](/symbols/yin-yang) hub explains the resulting relational principles, while the [Taiji Diagram](/symbols/taiji-diagram) examines their visual representation.

# Historical Context

The terms Taiji and Wuji appear in Chinese philosophical and cosmological traditions. They became especially important in later diagrams and commentaries that connected Taoist and Neo-Confucian thought.

The Taiji Diagram became one of the most recognizable visual expressions of this cosmology, showing the relationship between Yin and Yang within a larger whole.

# Wuji

Wuji suggests limitlessness, absence of fixed polarity, or undifferentiated potential. It points toward a state before distinctions such as active and receptive, bright and dark, movement and stillness.

In Taoist interpretation, Wuji may be discussed as close to the mystery of the Dao, though the terms are not always identical.

# Taiji

Taiji represents the emergence of structured polarity. It is not simply one thing among others. It is a principle of differentiation, the point at which Yin and Yang become distinguishable while remaining interdependent.

Taiji is therefore central to Yin Yang cosmology.

# Relationship to Yin Yang

Yin and Yang are the primary expression of Taiji. The Taiji Diagram shows each containing the seed of the other, suggesting movement, alternation, and mutual dependence.

This makes Taiji and Wuji foundational for understanding many archive topics, including Bagua, qi, Five Elements, and cosmic order.

# Relationship to Qi

Qi can be interpreted as the dynamic movement through which polarity and transformation appear. Taiji provides a symbolic structure for differentiation, while qi gives language to movement and vitality.

For the relationship between polarity and qi, see [Qi and Yin Yang](/taoism/qi-and-yin-yang). For the wider organization of world, season, direction, and order, continue to [Yin-Yang Cosmology](/taoism/yin-yang-cosmology).

# Cultural Significance

Taiji and Wuji are key concepts because they connect abstract Taoist cosmology with visible symbols. They help explain how the archive's entries on Dao, Yin Yang, Taiji Diagram, Bagua, and Five Elements belong to a shared cosmological field.
