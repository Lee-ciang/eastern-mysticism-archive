# Overview

Deity symbolism refers to the visual signs, attributes, colors, objects, animals, gestures, robes, weapons, and ritual settings associated with Chinese deities.

These symbols help identify divine figures and communicate their roles.

This archive treats deity imagery as cultural, religious, and artistic symbolism.

It does not present symbols as factual proof of divine power.

# Images and Attributes

Chinese deities may be identified through robes, crowns, tablets, weapons, animals, attendants, facial expressions, or posture.

The `jade-emperor`, `three-pure-ones`, `thunder-god`, `wealth-god`, and `star-deities` may each use different symbolic languages.

# Deity Roles

Symbols often communicate a deity's function.

A martial figure may carry weapons.

A heavenly ruler may wear imperial robes.

A household deity may appear in domestic imagery.

# Ritual Meaning

Symbols appear in temples, altar images, talismans, festival prints, and household shrines.

They connect deity imagery to `deity-offerings` and ritual practice.
