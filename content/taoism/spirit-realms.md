# Overview

Spirit realms are imagined domains of gods, ancestors, ghosts, underworld authorities, immortals, and other invisible beings in Chinese religious culture. The term is a modern archive label rather than a single traditional doctrine. It helps organize many related ideas about Heaven, Earth, underworld, ancestral space, ghostly movement, and ritual access.

Taoist cosmology often describes the world as layered, ordered, and relational. Spirit realms may appear as celestial heavens, terrestrial zones, mountain grottoes, underworld courts, ancestral domains, or ritual spaces opened through offerings and liturgy. Folk religious practice may treat these realms less systematically but still recognizes boundaries between the human world and unseen presences.

# Relation to the Three Realms

The Three Realms provide one of the most useful frameworks for understanding spirit realms. Heaven, Earth, and the human or underworld domains may be described in different ways depending on the tradition. The important point is that Chinese religious cosmology often imagines reality as more than a flat human environment.

Spirit realms are not only distant places. They may overlap with everyday life through temples, altars, graves, thresholds, festivals, and ritual time. A household altar can become a point of contact with ancestors. A temple can serve as a court of divine authority. A festival can mark the temporary opening of ghost gates.

# Administrative and Ritual Geography

Many spirit-world traditions organize realms through bureaucratic imagery. The celestial bureaucracy, underworld courts, City God offices, and Kitchen God reports all present invisible orders through the language of administration. This gives rituals a procedural structure: petitions may be submitted, offerings made, names registered, and protection requested.

Spirit realms also have ritual geography. Mountains, tombs, temples, doorways, crossroads, bridges, and thresholds may be treated as meaningful boundary zones. Protective practices often address these borders because they are imagined as places where ordinary and spirit-world conditions can meet.

# Ancestors and Wandering Spirits

Ancestor spirits and wandering spirits occupy different positions within spirit-realm thinking. Ancestors are linked to family continuity, tablets, offerings, and ritual memory. Wandering spirits may be associated with unsettled death, neglect, anonymity, or seasonal release during Ghost Month.

This distinction is not always absolute. Ritual practice often aims to give order to ambiguous situations, transforming danger, grief, or uncertainty into recognized forms of care.
