# Overview

The Queen Mother of the West, known as Xi Wangmu, is a major figure in Chinese mythology, immortality traditions, and Taoist religious imagination. She is often associated with the western paradise, immortality, sacred mountains, divine gatherings, and powerful feminine authority.

Her image changed over time, moving through mythic, religious, literary, and popular forms.

# Historical Context

Early references to the Queen Mother of the West present her as a powerful and sometimes wild figure. Later traditions increasingly connected her with immortality, celestial order, paradise imagery, and refined divine status.

In Taoist-influenced traditions, she became especially important in stories of immortals and sacred feasts.

# Immortality Associations

The Queen Mother of the West is often linked with peaches of immortality, divine banquets, and the company of immortals. These themes express the religious imagination of transcendence, longevity, and access to sacred realms.

The archive treats these as mythological and religious traditions, not as factual claims about immortality.

# Relationship to Taoist Immortals

Xi Wangmu is closely connected with Taoist immortal traditions. She may appear as a host, patron, or celestial figure in stories involving immortals and sacred realms.

This makes her a central bridge between deity studies and the immortals cluster.

# Celestial Geography

The western paradise or western realm associated with the Queen Mother belongs to a symbolic geography of sacred space. It links Heaven, Earth, mountains, and mythic direction.

This connects her to `taoist-heavens`, `three-realms`, and `heaven-and-earth`.

# Relationship to Taoist Cosmology

The Queen Mother of the West shows how Taoist cosmology is not only abstract. It can be expressed through mythic places, divine figures, sacred fruits, and stories of transformation.

# Cultural Significance

The Queen Mother of the West is one of the most important feminine divine figures in the archive. She strengthens links between Taoist deities, immortals, mythology, sacred geography, and cosmology.
