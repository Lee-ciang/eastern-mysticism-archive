# Overview

Immortals in Taoism are sacred or extraordinary figures associated with transcendence, cultivation, longevity, mountain retreats, and religious imagination.

They appear in Taoist stories, temple imagery, art, popular religion, and symbolic literature.

This page treats immortals as cultural and religious figures.

It does not present immortality claims as factual or medical truth.

# Place Among Deities

Immortals overlap with deity traditions but are not always identical to gods.

Some are remembered as perfected humans, sages, adepts, or legendary figures.

They belong to the wider `chinese-deities` and `taoist-deities` cluster.

# Cultivation Themes

Immortal traditions often connect to cultivation, discipline, nature, mountains, and transformation.

These themes relate to `dao`, `qi-energy`, `yin-yang`, and `five-elements`.

The archive avoids medical or supernatural claims.

# Heavenly Context

Immortals may be imagined in relation to heavens, divine courts, sacred mountains, and celestial realms.

# Symbolism

Immortals may be shown with peaches, staffs, gourds, cranes, robes, mountains, or other symbols.

`deity-symbolism` explains how such attributes carry meaning.

# Offerings and Devotion

Some immortals may receive offerings in temples or popular devotion.

`deity-offerings` gives broader context for these practices.

# Cultural Role

Immortals express ideals of transcendence, harmony with nature, spiritual discipline, and freedom from ordinary limits.

They are important in art, folklore, literature, and religious practice.
