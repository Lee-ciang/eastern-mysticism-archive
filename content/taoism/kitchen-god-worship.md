# Overview

Kitchen God worship refers to household customs honoring the Kitchen God, often known as Zao Jun.

The Kitchen God is a domestic deity in Chinese folk religion.

He is associated with the home, kitchen, household conduct, and reports to Heaven in popular tradition.

This page treats Kitchen God worship as cultural and historical practice.

# Household Context

Kitchen God customs belong to household religion.

They connect strongly to [Kitchen God](/folk-beliefs/kitchen-god), [Household Altar](/folk-beliefs/household-altar), [Ancestor Veneration](/folk-beliefs/ancestor-veneration), and [Festival Offerings](/folk-beliefs/festival-offerings).

The kitchen becomes a ritual space because it is connected with nourishment, family life, and domestic order.

# Relationship to Chinese Deities

The Kitchen God is one of the most familiar household deities.

He belongs to the wider `chinese-deities` cluster.

He also overlaps with `taoist-deities` and `deities-in-taoism` through shared ritual language.

# Celestial Reporting

Popular tradition often describes the Kitchen God reporting household behavior to Heaven.

This connects the figure to `jade-emperor` and `celestial-bureaucracy`.

The archive treats this as religious and folk belief, not as factual heavenly administration.

# Festival Customs

Kitchen God worship is often discussed around New Year customs.

Offerings may be made before the end of the lunar year.

The practice links household ritual to festival time and family renewal.

The Kitchen God symbolizes domestic morality, family order, and the relationship between household life and cosmic authority.
