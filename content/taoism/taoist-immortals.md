# Overview

Taoist immortals, often called xian, are figures associated with transcendence, longevity, spiritual attainment, sacred mountains, celestial realms, and extraordinary transformation. They appear in Taoist religion, Chinese mythology, literature, visual art, and popular culture.

Immortals are not all the same type of being. Some are legendary sages, some are mythic figures, some are transformed adepts, and some belong to devotional or literary traditions.

# Historical Context

Immortality traditions developed early in Chinese religious history. They were connected with mountains, elixirs, longevity practices, meditation, alchemy, sacred journeys, and stories of ascent beyond ordinary human limits.

Taoist traditions gave these ideas rich religious and symbolic forms.

# Xian as Transcendent Figures

The term xian is often translated as immortal, but it can suggest a transformed or transcendent being rather than simply someone who lives forever. Immortals may dwell in sacred mountains, celestial realms, or paradisal settings.

The archive treats these figures as religious and mythological traditions, not as factual biographies.

# Relationship to Qi Cultivation

Some immortality traditions connect xian with practices of cultivating qi, refining the body, or aligning with the Dao. These ideas vary widely by lineage and text.

This creates a bridge between deity studies, qi practice, and Taoist cosmology.

# Queen Mother of the West

The Queen Mother of the West is strongly linked with immortality traditions. Stories of peaches of immortality, divine banquets, and western sacred realms make her a major figure in the immortal imagination.

She is an important related node for this entry.

# Lord Lao and Sacred Teaching

Lord Lao may also connect to immortality through Taoist teachings, alchemical symbolism, and religious authority. His role as a divine teacher helps link immortals to Taoist theology.

# Cultural Significance

Taoist immortals expand the deity cluster beyond gods and officials. They connect mythology, cultivation, qi, sacred geography, Queen Mother of the West, Lord Lao, and Taoist heavens.
