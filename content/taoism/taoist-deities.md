# Overview

Taoist deities are divine figures honored, invoked, represented, or discussed within religious Taoism and related Chinese religious culture.

They may appear in temples, liturgies, altar images, talismans, ritual registers, festival rites, and devotional practice.

This page connects strongly to `deities-in-taoism`, `jade-emperor`, `three-pure-ones`, and `celestial-bureaucracy`.

# Religious Context

Taoism includes philosophical texts about the Dao and religious systems with gods, scriptures, rituals, priests, registers, and temples.

Taoist deities belong especially to religious Taoism.

They can also overlap with Chinese folk religion.

# Divine Hierarchy

Many Taoist traditions imagine divine order through hierarchy.

The `jade-emperor` may appear as a high ruler in popular religious imagination.

The `three-pure-ones` represent elevated Taoist theological figures.

The `celestial-bureaucracy` page explains the administrative model used for many divine offices.

# Relation to the Dao

The Dao is not simply one deity among others.

The page `dao` explains the underlying way or principle of reality.

Taoist deities are understood within a cosmos shaped by Dao, ritual order, and symbolic transformation.

# Ritual and Temple Practice

Taoist deities may receive offerings, incense, petitions, talismans, and ritual attention.

`deity-offerings` explains offering practices.

`deity-symbolism` explains images and attributes.

# Cultural Framing

This article treats deity traditions as cultural and religious history.

It does not treat claims about divine power as factual proof.
