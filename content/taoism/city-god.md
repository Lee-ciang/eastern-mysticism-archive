# Overview

The City God, often known as Cheng Huang, is a local protective deity associated with a city, district, or community. City God temples were important in many Chinese communities, where the deity could be honored as a guardian of local order, justice, and spiritual administration.

The City God belongs to the shared world of Chinese folk religion and Taoist-influenced temple culture.

# Historical Context

City God worship developed within a religious imagination that connected local government, moral order, and sacred protection. The City God was often understood through bureaucratic language, similar to officials in an earthly administration.

This made the City God a local counterpart to broader celestial bureaucracy.

# Local Protection

The City God may be associated with protecting a city, overseeing local spirits, maintaining order, and responding to community concerns. In popular religion, City God temples could become important centers for offerings, festivals, and ritual consultation.
