# Overview

The Three Realms is a broad way of describing layered worlds or domains in Taoist and Chinese religious cosmology. Depending on the source, the phrase may refer to celestial, earthly, and underworld domains, or to other structured levels of existence.

Because Taoist traditions are diverse, the Three Realms should not be treated as one fixed universal map. It is better understood as a category of cosmological organization.

# Historical Context

Religious Taoism developed complex models of heavens, divine administrations, sacred registers, underworld offices, and ritual pathways. These models were shaped by Taoist scripture, liturgy, local religion, Buddhist influence, and Chinese bureaucratic imagination.

The idea of multiple realms helped ritual specialists describe communication between human communities and sacred or unseen domains.

# Celestial Realm

The celestial realm may be associated with heavens, deities, stars, immortals, sacred offices, or cosmic authority. Figures such as the Three Pure Ones belong to high Taoist theological systems.

This realm expresses order, purity, and sacred hierarchy in many religious Taoist contexts.

# Earthly Realm

The earthly realm is the domain of human life, mountains, rivers, homes, temples, and ritual communities. It is not separate from sacred order. Rituals, offerings, talismans, and festivals often occur in earthly settings while addressing larger cosmological relationships.

Feng Shui also interprets earthly space through qi and pattern.

# Underworld and Spirit Domains

Some Chinese religious systems describe underworld courts, ancestors, ghosts, or other spirit domains. These are especially relevant to ancestor veneration, funerary ritual, Ghost Month, and offerings for the dead.

The archive describes these as religious beliefs and symbolic systems, not as factual geography.

# Ritual Significance

The Three Realms concept helps explain why Taoist ritual can involve petitions, offerings, registers, invocations, and symbolic communication across levels of reality.

Ritual action often depends on a cosmological map.

# Cultural Significance

Three Realms is important because it connects Taoist cosmology with ritual, ancestors, spirit entities, temples, and the organization of sacred space. It broadens the cluster beyond abstract philosophy into religious worldview.
