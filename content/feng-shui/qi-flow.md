# Overview

Qi flow is a central idea in Feng Shui. It refers to the way qi is interpreted as moving, gathering, dispersing, or becoming blocked within a landscape, building, room, or path.

In this archive, qi flow is discussed as a cultural and symbolic concept within Feng Shui, not as a measurable scientific force.

# Spatial Meaning

Feng Shui often interprets space through movement. Doors, corridors, windows, roads, water, mountains, furniture, and open areas may all be described in terms of how they influence flow.

A space may be seen as calm, rushed, stagnant, exposed, supported, or balanced depending on its arrangement.

# Landscape Context

Classical Feng Shui paid close attention to landforms. Mountains, rivers, ridges, valleys, and watercourses were interpreted as shaping the movement of qi through the environment.

Dragon imagery sometimes appears in this context as a metaphor for landform movement and vitality.

# Interior Context

In interior Feng Shui, qi flow may refer to how people and attention move through a space. Sharp corners, blocked paths, clutter, or overly direct alignments may be interpreted as affecting the quality of flow.

These interpretations are traditional and symbolic. They should not be treated as guaranteed methods for producing specific outcomes.

# Relationship to the Bagua

Qi flow is often discussed alongside Bagua maps and directional systems. The Bagua may provide a symbolic framework, while qi flow describes movement and spatial quality.

Together, these ideas help explain why Feng Shui is concerned with both orientation and arrangement.

# Cultural Significance

Qi flow is one of the most important concepts for understanding Feng Shui. It connects cosmology, landscape, architecture, and everyday spatial practice.
