# Overview

Qi in Feng Shui refers to the way Feng Shui traditions interpret movement, vitality, atmosphere, and spatial quality. The term qi provides a language for discussing how places are perceived as supportive, blocked, exposed, calm, active, stagnant, or balanced.

This archive treats qi in Feng Shui as a cultural and symbolic concept, not as a measured scientific force.

# Historical Context

Feng Shui developed from Chinese concerns with landscape, burial sites, settlement, orientation, water, mountains, wind, and environmental relationship. Qi became one of the central concepts for describing how these factors were interpreted.

Classical Feng Shui often considered landforms, watercourses, directions, and compass readings. Modern popular Feng Shui may focus more on interior arrangement and symbolic maps.

# Spatial Meaning

In Feng Shui, qi is often discussed as moving through or gathering within a place. A road, hallway, doorway, river, window, or open room can be interpreted as affecting flow.

The idea is not only movement. Qi can also be described as settling, dispersing, rushing, stagnating, or being contained.

# Relationship to Qi Flow

`qi-flow` is the applied spatial expression of qi in Feng Shui. A page on qi itself explains the broader concept, while qi flow explains how Feng Shui applies that concept to space.

This distinction helps the archive avoid making qi and Feng Shui identical. Qi appears in Taoist thought, cosmology, and cultivation as well.

# Relationship to Bagua

The Bagua can provide a symbolic structure for interpreting qi in space. Bagua maps, directions, trigrams, and Five Elements correspondences may be used to organize spatial meaning.

In this sense, Bagua and qi work together: qi describes movement and quality, while Bagua provides a diagrammatic framework.

# Relationship to Five Elements

Feng Shui often interprets qi through the Five Elements. A space may be described through Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, or Water qualities. These symbolic categories can express activity, stability, flow, structure, or growth.

Different schools use these associations differently.

# Balanced Qi

Balanced qi in Feng Shui usually refers to a symbolic sense of proportion and suitable movement. Too much direct movement may be interpreted as harsh, while too little movement may be interpreted as stagnant.

The archive does not present these ideas as guaranteed methods for wealth, health, or luck.

# Cultural Significance

Qi in Feng Shui is a major bridge between cosmology and spatial practice. It connects the archive's entries on qi, Bagua, Five Elements, Lo Shu, Luopan, and environmental symbolism.
