# Overview

Cosmology and Feng Shui are closely related because Feng Shui applies Chinese cosmological ideas to space, landscape, orientation, and environmental relationship. Concepts such as qi, Yin Yang, Five Elements, Bagua, Heaven and Earth, and directional symbolism all shape Feng Shui interpretation.

This archive presents Feng Shui as a cultural, historical, and symbolic spatial tradition, not as a guaranteed method for producing outcomes.

# Historical Context

Feng Shui developed from Chinese concerns with settlement, burial sites, landforms, water, wind, direction, and the relationship between human life and environment. Over time, cosmological systems became central to its vocabulary.

Classical Feng Shui traditions often used compass methods, landform analysis, calendrical systems, and symbolic correspondences. Modern popular Feng Shui may simplify these systems into interior design frameworks.

# Qi and Landscape

Qi is one of Feng Shui's central concepts. Landforms, rivers, roads, doors, windows, and room arrangements may be interpreted through qi flow.

In cosmological terms, qi links environment with vitality, movement, and atmosphere. The archive treats this as traditional interpretation rather than scientific measurement.

# Yin Yang in Space

Yin Yang provides a language for spatial balance. A place may be interpreted as too exposed or too enclosed, too active or too still, too bright or too dark.

Feng Shui uses such contrasts to discuss relational balance rather than simple decoration.

# Five Elements

The Five Elements help classify colors, materials, forms, directions, and qualities of movement. Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water can be used to interpret how a space feels or functions symbolically.

This connects Feng Shui directly to Wu Xing cosmology.

# Bagua

The Bagua provides a diagrammatic framework for applying cosmology to space. Bagua maps and directional systems connect trigrams, elements, areas, and movement.

This is why Bagua entries should link strongly to Feng Shui entries.

# Heaven, Earth, and Human

Feng Shui expresses the relationship between Heaven, Earth, and Human by interpreting how human dwellings fit into larger patterns of time, landscape, and direction.

This makes it one of the archive's clearest examples of applied cosmology.

# Cultural Significance

Cosmology and Feng Shui is a major bridge page between the Taoist Cosmology Cluster and existing Feng Shui content. It connects qi, Bagua, Five Elements, qi flow, and spatial interpretation into one applied framework.
