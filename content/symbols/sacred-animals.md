# Overview

Sacred animals are mythological, symbolic, and culturally significant creatures in Chinese traditions.

They appear in art, ritual objects, architecture, cosmology, folklore, imperial imagery, and protective symbolism.

This page is the hub for the Chinese Sacred Animals Entity Cluster.

It treats sacred creatures as cultural and historical symbols.

It does not present mythological beings as factual zoological realities.

# Mythological Animals

Chinese sacred animal traditions include dragons, phoenix-like birds, Qilin, tigers, tortoises, and directional beasts.

Their meanings differ by period, region, religion, artistic setting, and ritual use.

# Dragon and Phoenix

`dragon-symbolism` explores dragon imagery in cosmology, authority, water, and vitality.

`phoenix-symbolism` examines renewal, virtue, harmony, and courtly imagery.

The existing `dragon-symbol` page provides additional historical context.

# Qilin

The `qilin-symbol` page covers a composite auspicious creature associated with virtue, peace, and exceptional events.

Its appearance in stories and art is symbolic rather than biological.

# Four Sacred Beasts

`four-sacred-beasts` introduces the Azure Dragon, White Tiger, Vermilion Bird, and Black Tortoise.

These creatures are connected with directions, seasons, colors, and cosmological order.

# Guardian Creatures

`guardian-creatures` explains how animal forms can protect gates, tombs, temples, roofs, and households symbolically.

Related authority pages include `guardian-symbols` and `protective-charms`.

# Cosmological Context

Sacred animals often connect to `bagua`, `yin-yang`, `five-elements`, `qi-energy`, and `cosmology-in-taoism`.

These systems provide a symbolic framework for direction, balance, phase, and cosmic relationship.

# Zodiac Context

Some sacred animals overlap with `chinese-zodiac` and `zodiac-symbolism`.
