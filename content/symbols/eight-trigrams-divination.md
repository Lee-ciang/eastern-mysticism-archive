# Overview

Eight Trigrams divination refers to the interpretive use of the eight basic trigrams associated with the I Ching and Bagua symbolism. Each trigram is made of three lines and can be connected with natural images, directions, family roles, movement patterns, and symbolic qualities.

This page connects the new I Ching / Divination Cluster to existing archive pages such as `eight-trigrams`, `bagua`, and `lo-shu-square`.

# The Eight Trigrams

The eight trigrams are formed from combinations of broken and unbroken lines. These line forms are commonly associated with Yin and Yang. In traditional interpretation, the trigrams may correspond to images such as Heaven, Earth, Thunder, Wind, Water, Fire, Mountain, and Lake.

The page `trigrams` introduces the basic structure, while `eight-trigrams-divination` focuses on how those structures can be read in an oracle or interpretive context.

# Trigrams in Hexagrams

Every I Ching `hexagrams` figure contains two trigrams. A diviner may read the lower trigram, upper trigram, their relationship, and any `changing-lines` that alter the figure.

This layered structure gives a reading more depth. It is not only a six-line figure; it is also a relationship between two three-line images.

# Bagua and Divination

The `bagua` diagram arranges the eight trigrams into a meaningful pattern. This arrangement became important in cosmology, Feng Shui, and symbolic interpretation. While Bagua systems are not identical to I Ching consultation, they share trigram foundations.

The existing `eight-trigrams` page helps bridge these traditions. It shows how a divinatory symbol can also become a cosmological diagram.
