# Overview

Casting coins is a widely used method for consulting the I Ching. It usually involves tossing three coins six times to form the six lines of a hexagram. Each toss produces a line value, and some values may indicate changing lines.

This method is popular because it is simple, portable, and easy to learn. It belongs to the broader topic of `divination-methods` and is often contrasted with the more elaborate `yarrow-stalk-method`.

# How Coin Casting Functions

In a typical coin method, heads and tails are assigned numerical values. The total of the three coins determines whether a line is Yin or Yang and whether it is stable or changing. The process is repeated from the bottom line upward until a full hexagram is formed.

The resulting figure is interpreted through `hexagrams`, `changing-lines`, and sometimes the relationship between upper and lower `trigrams`.

# Changing Lines and Movement

Coin casting can produce changing lines. These lines are important because they show movement within the reading. A changing Yin line may become Yang, and a changing Yang line may become Yin. This can transform one hexagram into another.

This movement connects coin casting to `yin-yang` and `yin-and-yang-balance`, where change is understood as relational transformation rather than fixed fate.

# Method and Ritual Frame

Although coin casting is simple, it can still be treated as a ritualized act. The questioner may pause, formulate a question, cast the coins carefully, record the line values, and interpret the result within a serious frame.

This ritual frame connects coin casting to `oracle-traditions`. The method is not only about chance. It creates a symbolic encounter between question, timing, and interpretation.

# Comparison with Yarrow Stalks

The `yarrow-stalk-method` is slower and more traditional. Coin casting is quicker and more accessible. Both methods generate hexagrams and changing lines, but they differ in pace, texture, and historical association.

The archive treats both as cultural practices rather than instructions promising supernatural certainty.

# Cosmological Context

Coin casting uses simple objects, but the symbolic system behind it is complex. The lines connect to `yin-yang`; trigrams connect to `bagua` and `eight-trigrams`; hexagrams connect to the `king-wen-sequence`; and the whole practice belongs to a worldview shaped by `dao`, `cosmology-in-taoism`, and `qi-energy`.
