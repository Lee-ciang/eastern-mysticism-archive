# Overview

Guardian creatures are animal or composite forms used symbolically to protect spaces, boundaries, tombs, temples, homes, and sacred objects.

They appear in architecture, sculpture, prints, ritual art, and folk belief.

This archive treats guardian creatures as cultural and protective symbols.

It does not present them as sources of guaranteed supernatural protection.

These pages explain symbolic protection in different settings.

# Gates and Thresholds

Guardian creatures may be placed near gates, doors, bridges, roofs, or entrances.

Their position marks a boundary between inside and outside.

# Temple and Tomb Art

Animal guardians may appear in temples and tombs.

They can symbolize watchfulness, rank, sacred order, or protection of the dead.

# Sacred Animals

The wider `sacred-animals` hub includes dragons, phoenix imagery, Qilin, and directional beasts.

Not every sacred animal functions as a guardian in every context.

`dragon-symbolism` and `qilin-symbol` can overlap with guardian architecture and auspicious display.
