# Overview

Trigrams are three-line symbols made from broken and unbroken lines. In the I Ching, they form the building blocks of hexagrams. In broader Chinese symbolism, they appear in the Bagua, directional systems, cosmological diagrams, Feng Shui, and later interpretive traditions.

Each trigram can be read as a pattern of Yin and Yang. Because there are three line positions and two possible line types, the system produces eight basic trigrams. These eight forms are central to the I Ching / Divination Cluster and connect directly to existing archive pages such as `eight-trigrams`, `bagua`, and `lo-shu-square`.

# Trigrams and Yin-Yang Structure

The lines of a trigram are often interpreted through Yin and Yang. A broken line is commonly associated with Yin, and an unbroken line with Yang. The combination of three lines creates a symbolic image with a distinct quality.

This links `trigrams` to `yin-yang` and `yin-and-yang-balance`. A trigram is not merely a mark. It is a small diagram of relation, movement, and tendency.

# Trigrams in Hexagrams

In the I Ching, each `hexagrams` page topic can be understood as two stacked trigrams. The lower trigram and upper trigram interact to create a more complex symbolic situation. A hexagram therefore contains both a six-line structure and two three-line images.

This makes trigrams essential for interpreting hexagrams. They provide images such as Heaven, Earth, Thunder, Wind, Water, Fire, Mountain, and Lake in many traditional readings.

# Bagua and Cosmology

The Bagua is a diagrammatic arrangement of eight trigrams. Existing pages such as `bagua`, `eight-trigrams`, and `lo-shu-square` show how trigram symbolism moved into wider cosmological and spatial systems.

In Feng Shui and later symbolic thought, trigrams may be associated with directions, family roles, natural forces, and phases of change. This does not erase their I Ching role; it expands their cultural meaning.

# Relationship to Dao and Qi

Trigrams belong to a worldview in which reality is patterned and changing. This connects them to `dao`, `cosmology-in-taoism`, and `qi-energy`. The Dao provides the broader idea of natural unfolding, while qi and Yin-Yang describe movement, relation, and vitality.

Trigrams are one symbolic language for representing those patterns.
