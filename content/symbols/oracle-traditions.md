# Overview

Oracle traditions are cultural practices that seek guidance through signs, patterns, ritual methods, or symbolic interpretation. In Chinese culture, the I Ching became one of the most influential oracle systems, using hexagrams, trigrams, changing lines, and interpretive texts to explore situations and change.

This page places `i-ching-divination` within a wider context of divination, symbolic reading, and classical cosmology.

# Oracle Practice and Interpretation

An oracle tradition depends on more than a method. It requires a question, a ritual frame, a sign-producing process, and a system of interpretation. In the I Ching, the sign is usually a hexagram generated through a method such as `casting-coins` or the `yarrow-stalk-method`.

The reading is then interpreted through `hexagrams`, `trigrams`, `changing-lines`, and textual tradition.

# The I Ching as Oracle

The I Ching is not simply a list of predictions. It is a symbolic system for thinking through change, conduct, timing, danger, restraint, and opportunity. Its oracle function depends on the idea that a generated pattern can speak meaningfully to a situation.

This makes I Ching divination both practical and philosophical. It connects action to reflection.

# Methods and Ritual Seriousness

The page `divination-methods` explains the procedures used to create an I Ching reading. The method provides discipline. It slows the questioner down, creates a formal moment, and produces a symbolic figure.

This ritual seriousness helps distinguish oracle consultation from casual guessing.

# Cosmological Background

Chinese oracle traditions are often connected with cosmology. The I Ching uses line polarity related to `yin-yang`, trigram symbolism related to `bagua` and `eight-trigrams`, and a view of change that resonates with `dao`, `cosmology-in-taoism`, `qi-energy`, and `yin-and-yang-balance`.

Other symbolic systems, such as `lo-shu-square` and `five-elements`, show how Chinese traditions often read number, direction, phase, and pattern as meaningful.

# Oracle and Cultural Life

Oracle traditions could be used for personal reflection, political decisions, ritual timing, family questions, travel, danger, and moral uncertainty. The archive treats these practices as cultural and historical systems rather than claims of guaranteed prediction.

The value of studying oracle traditions lies in understanding how people used symbolic structures to reason about uncertainty.
