# Overview

The Wood Element, or Wood Phase, is one of the Five Elements in Chinese cosmology. The Chinese system is often called Wu Xing, which is more accurately understood as Five Phases or Five Movements rather than five fixed substances.

Wood is traditionally associated with growth, expansion, springtime, upward movement, flexibility, renewal, and the living quality of plants and trees. It is not simply literal wood. It is a symbolic category used to describe a mode of transformation.

# Historical Context

The Five Phases system developed in early Chinese correlative thought and became important in cosmology, ritual, medicine, music, governance, astrology, Feng Shui, and symbolic interpretation. Wood became one phase within a larger network of seasonal and directional relationships.

Because Wu Xing was used across many traditions, the Wood Element should not be treated as belonging to one school alone. It appears in Taoist interpretation, Feng Shui, divination systems, and broader Chinese symbolic culture.

# Symbolic Meaning

Wood represents generative movement. It suggests sprouting, branching, reaching, and beginning. Its imagery often comes from trees, shoots, bamboo, forests, and spring growth.

Symbolically, Wood may indicate development, planning, vitality, and the first visible emergence of a process. These meanings are interpretive and cultural rather than scientific claims.

# Place in the Generating Cycle

In the generating cycle, Water nourishes Wood, and Wood feeds Fire. This sequence expresses a symbolic relationship of support. Water allows plants to grow, and Wood provides fuel for flame.

The generating cycle is not a literal chemical explanation. It is a traditional model for thinking about transformation and interdependence.

# Place in the Overcoming Cycle

In the overcoming cycle, Wood is traditionally said to control Earth, as roots can penetrate soil. Metal controls Wood, as an axe or blade can cut timber.

These images express restraint and regulation. They help the Five Phases function as a system of relationships rather than a list of isolated symbols.

# Feng Shui Context

In Feng Shui, Wood may be associated with growth, vertical forms, plants, green colors, and certain directional or Bagua correspondences depending on the system. It may be used to interpret the symbolic quality of a room or landscape.

This archive presents these associations as cultural and historical meanings, not guaranteed spatial outcomes.

# Relationship to Qi

Wood can be understood as one pattern through which qi is interpreted. Qi moving in a Wood-like pattern may be described as expanding, rising, or growing.

This makes Wood an important entry in the wider Qi and Five Elements cluster.
