# Overview

The King Wen sequence is the traditional ordering of the sixty-four I Ching hexagrams. It is one of the most influential ways the hexagrams have been arranged and interpreted. The sequence gives the hexagrams a relational structure, placing each figure in a meaningful order rather than treating the system as a random list.

This page belongs to the I Ching / Divination Cluster because sequence affects how readers understand change, pairing, contrast, reversal, and development.

# Sequence and Meaning

In the I Ching, order matters. The `hexagrams` can be read individually, but their sequence also suggests relationships. One hexagram may follow another, oppose another, complete another, or reverse another. These relationships can shape interpretation.

The King Wen sequence is therefore more than a table of contents. It is a symbolic arrangement.

# Pairing and Contrast

Many readings of the King Wen sequence pay attention to hexagram pairs. Pairs may involve inversion, contrast, complement, or transformation. This connects the sequence to `changing-lines`, because divination often moves from one hexagram to another through line change.

The result is a system where meaning can emerge from both a sign and its place among other signs.

# Trigrams and Hexagrams

The sequence orders `hexagrams`, but each hexagram is still built from `trigrams`. Understanding trigrams helps readers see internal structure within the sequence. The pages `trigrams`, `eight-trigrams-divination`, and the existing `eight-trigrams` page support this interpretive layer.

# Divination Methods

Methods such as `casting-coins` and the `yarrow-stalk-method` generate a hexagram. The King Wen sequence then provides the traditional numbering and ordering through which the result is located.

This makes the sequence practical as well as symbolic. It helps readers identify and interpret the generated figure.
