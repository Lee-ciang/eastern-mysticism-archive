# Overview

Guardian symbols are images, figures, animals, deities, or signs used to represent protection, boundary keeping, authority, and watchfulness. In Chinese folk religion and visual culture, guardian symbols appear at doors, temples, graves, bridges, household spaces, and ritual objects.

They are symbolic expressions of protection and order. This archive does not treat them as factual guardians.

# Historical Context

Guardian imagery developed across imperial art, temple iconography, folk religion, funerary art, Taoist symbolism, and household custom. Different figures could guard different kinds of spaces: homes, tombs, gates, temples, altars, or cities.

The protective meaning often depended on placement. A guardian at a doorway has a different context from a guardian in a temple mural or tomb carving.

# Door Gods

Door gods are one of the clearest examples of guardian symbols. Their paired placement at entrances creates a visual boundary and expresses protective watchfulness.

They show how guardian imagery can become part of household ritual and seasonal renewal.

# Animal and Mythic Guardians

Dragons, lions, tigers, phoenixes, tortoises, and other animals may carry protective or authoritative meanings in different contexts. Stone lions at gates, for example, are often interpreted as guardians of entrances.

These symbols should be understood through cultural and historical context rather than one universal meaning.

# Ritual and Temple Contexts

Guardian figures appear in temples, processions, and ritual settings. They may mark sacred authority, ward boundaries, or frame the entrance into a ritual space.

In Taoist and folk religious contexts, guardian symbolism can overlap with talismans, seals, and protective rites.

# Relationship to Evil Aversion

Guardian symbols often work alongside evil-aversion symbols. A fierce face, weapon, animal, or divine figure may be used to confront harmful influences within traditional belief.

The archive presents these as symbolic and religious forms.

# Cultural Significance

Guardian symbols are a core node in the Protective Practices Cluster. They connect door gods, dragon symbols, household protection, threshold rituals, protective charms, and temple iconography.
