# Overview

The Eight Trigrams are a symbolic set of three-line figures used in Chinese cosmology, the I Ching, Taoist thought, and Feng Shui. Each trigram is made from combinations of solid and broken lines. Solid lines are commonly associated with yang, while broken lines are associated with yin.

Together, the trigrams form a compact symbolic language for describing patterns of change, natural forces, family relationships, directions, and energetic qualities.

# Symbolic Structure

Each trigram contains three lines. Because each line may be yin or yang, there are eight possible combinations. These are traditionally associated with heaven, earth, thunder, wind, water, fire, mountain, and lake.

The trigrams are not simply decorative marks. In traditional interpretation, they represent relationships between movement and stillness, strength and receptivity, rising and descending, openness and containment.

# I Ching Context

The trigrams are central to the I Ching, where they combine into sixty-four hexagrams. In that context, they are used to describe changing conditions and symbolic situations.

The archive treats this material as cultural and historical symbolism rather than predictive instruction. The trigrams are important because they show how Chinese thought often organizes the world through patterned correspondences.

# Taoist and Cosmological Meaning

In Taoist and cosmological contexts, the trigrams are closely linked to yin-yang theory and the idea that reality unfolds through dynamic transformation. Each trigram expresses a different pattern of balance between yin and yang.

This makes the Eight Trigrams a useful bridge between abstract cosmology and visible symbols.

# Feng Shui Connection

The trigrams are also important in Feng Shui, especially through the Bagua map and directional systems. Each trigram may be associated with a direction, family role, element, season, or spatial quality depending on the interpretive school.

# Cultural Significance

The Eight Trigrams appear on diagrams, temple objects, talismans, martial arts traditions, Feng Shui tools, and philosophical texts. Their broad use makes them one of the foundational symbol systems in the archive.
