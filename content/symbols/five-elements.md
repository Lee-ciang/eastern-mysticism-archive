# Overview

The Five Elements system, known as Wu Xing, is a foundational concept in Chinese philosophy and cosmology. It describes the dynamic interactions between five elemental forces: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water.

# Historical Origins

Wu Xing theory emerged during early Chinese philosophical development and became deeply integrated into Taoist thought, traditional medicine, military strategy, astrology, and Feng Shui.

# Element Structure

The Five Elements include:

- Wood
- Fire
- Earth
- Metal
- Water

Each element represents energetic qualities, seasonal cycles, emotional states, colors, directions, and cosmic transformations.

# Symbolic Meaning

Rather than literal substances, the Five Elements symbolize patterns of movement, balance, generation, and control within nature and human life.

# Cultural Context

Wu Xing philosophy influences many traditional East Asian systems including medicine, martial arts, architecture, divination, and metaphysical practices.

# Modern Interpretation

Today, the Five Elements remain widely referenced in Feng Shui, holistic traditions, Chinese medicine, and cultural symbolism.
