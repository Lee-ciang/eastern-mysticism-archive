# Overview

Fu talismans are ritual writings or symbolic diagrams associated with Taoist ritual culture and Chinese folk religious practice. They may appear on yellow paper, red paper, cloth, wood, doors, altars, charms, or ritual documents. In many traditions, Fu are connected with protection, purification, blessings, communication with deities, and the symbolic ordering of unseen forces.

This archive treats Fu talismans as cultural and historical objects. It does not present protective or supernatural claims as factual guarantees. Their importance lies in how they express religious authority, ritual language, sacred writing, and symbolic protection.

# Ritual Writing and Form

Fu talismans often combine ordinary characters, stylized marks, deity names, seals, directional signs, and ritual script. The page `talisman-writing` explains this writing tradition, while `talisman-seals` focuses on seals and marks of authority. `talisman-paper` covers the material surface on which Fu may be written or printed.

The visual form can be difficult to read as ordinary text because the talisman is not simply a message. It is a ritual symbol.
