# Overview

Hexagrams are the six-line figures used in the I Ching. Each hexagram is made from broken and unbroken lines, and the full system contains sixty-four possible combinations. These figures are used in divination, textual interpretation, cosmological reflection, and symbolic analysis.

The hexagram is the central visual unit of the I Ching. While a single line may express Yin or Yang, and a trigram may express a basic natural image, a hexagram creates a more complex situation. It combines structure, position, movement, image, judgment, and possible transformation.

# Structure of a Hexagram

A hexagram has six lines read from bottom to top. Lines may be broken or unbroken. Broken lines are often associated with Yin, while unbroken lines are associated with Yang. In divination, some lines may also be changing lines, which can transform the first hexagram into a second hexagram.

This structure links `hexagrams` directly to `changing-lines`, `yin-yang`, and `yin-and-yang-balance`. A hexagram is not only a sign. It is a patterned field of relation.

# Trigrams Inside Hexagrams

Each hexagram can be understood as two trigrams: a lower trigram and an upper trigram. The page `trigrams` explains these three-line figures, while `eight-trigrams-divination` connects them to divinatory reading.

# Sixty-Four Situations

The sixty-four hexagrams are often read as symbolic situations. They may concern beginnings, difficulty, waiting, conflict, modesty, gathering, obstruction, return, transformation, and many other patterns of life. Their power lies in relational meaning rather than literal prediction alone.

This makes the I Ching useful as an interpretive system. The hexagram names a situation and invites reflection on timing, conduct, risk, and change.

# Sequence and Order

The `king-wen-sequence` is the best-known ordering of the sixty-four hexagrams. Sequence matters because it places each hexagram in relation to others. The order can suggest development, contrast, reversal, pairing, and cyclical movement.

This connects I Ching symbolism to the broader archive themes of `dao`, `cosmology-in-taoism`, and `qi-energy`, where reality is understood through process and transformation.

# Divination Methods

Hexagrams are generated through methods such as `casting-coins` and the `yarrow-stalk-method`. These belong to the broader topic of `divination-methods`. A method provides a ritualized way to move from a question to a symbolic figure.

The generated hexagram is then interpreted through text, line position, changing lines, trigrams, and the question's context.
