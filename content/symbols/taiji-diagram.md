# Overview

The Taiji Diagram is a symbolic representation of Yin and Yang within Chinese cosmology and Taoist philosophy. It visually expresses balance, duality, transformation, and cosmic harmony.

# Historical Background

The Taiji concept developed within ancient Chinese metaphysical traditions and later became central to Taoist philosophy, Neo-Confucian thought, and martial arts symbolism.

# Symbolic Structure

The diagram traditionally consists of:

- A circular form
- Black Yin region
- White Yang region
- Opposing dots within each half

This structure represents the continuous interaction and transformation between opposing yet interconnected forces.

# Philosophical Meaning

The Taiji Diagram illustrates that:

- Yin contains Yang
- Yang contains Yin
- Opposites transform into one another
- Absolute separation does not exist

# Taoist Interpretation

Within Taoism, the Taiji symbolizes the origin of cosmic movement and the balance underlying all natural phenomena.

# Cultural Influence

The symbol became widely recognized through martial arts, meditation traditions, Feng Shui systems, traditional medicine, and East Asian spiritual culture.
