# Overview

The Lo Shu Square is a traditional Chinese numerical diagram often described as a three-by-three magic square. It is associated with cosmology, numerology, Feng Shui, and later systems of symbolic correspondence.

In the classic arrangement, the numbers one through nine are placed so that each row, column, and diagonal adds to the same total. This mathematical feature gave the diagram lasting symbolic importance.

# Legendary Background

Traditional accounts connect the Lo Shu Square with a turtle emerging from the Luo River, bearing a pattern on its shell. This story is part of a broader mythic and cosmological tradition rather than a historical record.

The turtle image helped connect number, pattern, nature, and sacred order. For this reason, the Lo Shu became more than a numerical puzzle. It became a diagram of patterned harmony.

# Symbolic Meaning

The Lo Shu Square is often interpreted as a map of balance and ordered movement. Its numbers may be associated with directions, elements, stars, seasons, or energetic qualities depending on the system using it.

The square shows how number can function symbolically in Chinese thought. It organizes meaning through position, relationship, and pattern.

# Feng Shui Use

In Feng Shui, the Lo Shu Square influenced later spatial mapping systems, including some Bagua arrangements and flying star methods. It provides a numerical foundation for interpreting directional and temporal patterns.

Different Feng Shui schools use the Lo Shu in different ways, so it should not be treated as a single universal formula.

# Relationship to the Bagua

The Lo Shu Square is often discussed alongside the Bagua because both organize space through symbolic correspondences. The Bagua emphasizes trigrams and cosmological images, while the Lo Shu emphasizes numerical order.

Together, they form part of the symbolic background for many spatial and divinatory systems.

# Cultural Significance

The Lo Shu Square remains important in discussions of Chinese numerology, Feng Shui, and traditional cosmology. It is a foundational entry for understanding how diagrams can carry philosophical and ritual meaning.
