# Overview

I Ching divination refers to the use of the Book of Changes, or Yi Jing, as an oracle system based on hexagrams, trigrams, changing lines, symbolic images, and patterned transformation. It is one of the most influential divination traditions in Chinese culture and is closely connected with classical cosmology, Yin and Yang, Daoist interpretation, and later systems of symbolic thought.

In the Eastern Mysticism Archive, this page is the hub for the I Ching / Divination Cluster. It connects the new divination pages with existing symbol and cosmology pages such as Bagua, Eight Trigrams, Lo Shu Square, Yin Yang, Five Elements, Dao, Taoist cosmology, Qi Energy, and Yin and Yang Balance.

# Divination and Change

The I Ching is centered on change. Its hexagrams are not static signs. They describe situations, tendencies, transitions, tensions, and possible transformations. A reading traditionally asks how a situation is moving, what forces are present, and how one might respond with awareness.

This makes the I Ching different from a simple fortune-telling list. It is an interpretive system that uses symbolic structure to think through timing, relation, danger, restraint, opportunity, and return.

# Hexagrams and Trigrams

The sixty-four hexagrams are built from six lines. Each line may be broken or unbroken, and each hexagram can be understood as a combination of two trigrams. The `hexagrams` page introduces the larger symbolic units, while `trigrams` and `eight-trigrams-divination` explain the three-line forms that underlie the system.

The existing `eight-trigrams` and `bagua` pages are important because the I Ching tradition deeply influenced later trigram, Bagua, and cosmological symbolism.

# Changing Lines

`changing-lines` are central to I Ching divination. A line may be stable or moving. When a line changes, it can transform one hexagram into another, giving the reading a dynamic structure.

This idea connects the oracle to `yin-yang`, because broken and unbroken lines can be understood through Yin and Yang qualities. It also connects to `yin-and-yang-balance`, where change is read as relational rather than fixed.

# Methods of Consultation

Traditional and modern methods include the `yarrow-stalk-method`, `casting-coins`, and other `divination-methods`. These methods create a hexagram through a ritualized process. The method is not merely mechanical. It frames the consultation as a serious interpretive act.
