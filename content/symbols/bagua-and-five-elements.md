# Overview

Bagua and Five Elements refers to the relationship between the Eight Trigrams and the Five Phases, also known as Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water. Together, these systems form one of the most important networks of correspondence in Chinese cosmology and Feng Shui.

The Bagua provides trigram structure. The Five Elements provide a language of process, transformation, and relational balance.

This symbols page focuses on the conceptual and visual correspondence between trigrams and phases. It does not provide room-by-room Feng Shui advice. For applied spatial interpretation, use [Five Elements and Bagua](/feng-shui/five-elements-and-bagua).

# Historical Context

Both the Bagua and Five Elements developed within broader Chinese systems of correlative thought. These systems linked natural forces, directions, seasons, numbers, colors, organs, social roles, and ritual meanings.

Over time, the Bagua and Five Elements were layered together in Feng Shui, divination, ritual diagrams, and cosmological interpretation. Their relationship is not a single fixed formula but a family of correspondences used in different contexts.

# The Bagua Side of the Relationship

The Bagua consists of eight trigrams made from yin and yang lines. Each trigram carries an image such as Heaven, Earth, Thunder, Wind, Water, Fire, Mountain, or Lake.

These trigrams can be arranged in different ways. The Early Heaven Bagua emphasizes primordial balance, while the Later Heaven Bagua emphasizes movement, direction, and the world of active transformation.

# The Five Elements Side

The Five Elements, or Wu Xing, are better understood as Five Phases. They describe modes of change and relationship: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water.

Each phase may be associated with directions, seasons, colors, shapes, materials, and qualities of movement. In Feng Shui, these associations are often used to interpret spatial balance.

The [Five Elements](/symbols/five-elements) authority hub explains why these are phases rather than five fixed material substances. The [Generating Cycle](/symbols/generating-cycle) and [Overcoming Cycle](/symbols/overcoming-cycle) explain the support and regulation sequences.

# Directional Correspondence

The link between Bagua and Five Elements is especially visible in directional systems. A trigram may be associated with a direction, and that direction may also be associated with an element.

For example, some systems connect east with Wood, south with Fire, west with Metal, north with Water, and central or transitional positions with Earth. Different schools may use more detailed mappings.

# Trigram and Phase Associations

In a widespread Later Heaven arrangement:

- Zhen, Thunder, and Xun, Wind or Wood, are associated with Wood.
- Li, Fire, is associated with Fire.
- Kun, Earth, and Gen, Mountain, are associated with Earth.
- Qian, Heaven, and Dui, Lake or Marsh, are associated with Metal.
- Kan, Water, is associated with Water.

The assignments do not erase trigram identity. Qian and Dui can both be assigned Metal while retaining different line structures, images, family roles, and meanings. A phase describes one relationship within a larger trigram system.

# Early Heaven and Later Heaven Context

The Early Heaven arrangement emphasizes paired opposition and an image of primordial order. The Later Heaven arrangement emphasizes directional, seasonal, and manifested change. Five Phase mappings used in Feng Shui are most often discussed through the Later Heaven sequence.

Readers should still identify the chart in use. Mixing positions from one sequence with assumptions from another produces diagrams that look authoritative but combine incompatible conventions. [Later Heaven Bagua](/symbols/later-heaven-bagua) and [Bagua Directions](/symbols/bagua-directions) provide the necessary orientation.

# Feng Shui Interpretation

In Feng Shui, Bagua and Five Elements analysis may be used to discuss the symbolic qualities of a room, building, landform, or object. A space might be interpreted through direction, element, material, color, or the perceived movement of qi.

The archive presents these as cultural and symbolic systems, not as guaranteed methods for producing health, wealth, or protection.

# Relationship to Qi

Qi is often the underlying concept connecting Bagua and Five Elements. The Bagua structures symbolic space, while the Five Elements describe phases of movement and transformation. Qi provides the language of flow and vitality within that system.

This relationship helps explain why Bagua, elements, and qi frequently appear together in Feng Shui writing.

The systems answer different questions. [Qi](/taoism/qi) names movement, atmosphere, and material vitality. Bagua differentiates eight configurations. Wu Xing differentiates five phase movements. Later traditions coordinate them through correspondence rather than treating them as synonyms.

# Cultural Significance

The relationship between Bagua and Five Elements is foundational for the archive because it links symbols, cosmology, Feng Shui, Taoist thought, and spatial interpretation. It is one of the most important bridges between abstract diagrams and applied cultural practice.

# Variation and Limits

Bagua and Wu Xing did not emerge as one inseparable chart at a single date. Their coordination developed historically, and technical traditions may add numbers, stems, branches, directions, time periods, or [Lo Shu Square](/symbols/lo-shu-square) positions.

No diagram guarantees health, wealth, relationships, or protection. The archive treats these maps as historical and symbolic knowledge systems.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Why are there eight trigrams but five phases?

They classify change differently. Later systems assign the same phase to more than one trigram where appropriate.

## Is a trigram identical to its assigned phase?

No. Phase correspondence is one layer of meaning. Each trigram retains its own line pattern, natural image, family role, and position.

## Which Bagua sequence is usually mapped to the Five Phases?

The Later Heaven arrangement is especially important for directional and seasonal correspondence.
