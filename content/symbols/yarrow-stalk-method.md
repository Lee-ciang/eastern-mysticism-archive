# Overview

The yarrow stalk method is a traditional procedure for consulting the I Ching. It uses a set of stalks, counted and divided through repeated operations, to generate the six lines of a hexagram. The method is slower and more ceremonial than `casting-coins`, and it is often associated with classical oracle practice.

In the archive, the yarrow stalk method is treated as a cultural and historical divination method. It is not presented as a guaranteed way to predict events.

# Ritual Pace

The yarrow stalk method matters partly because of its pace. It requires repeated handling, counting, separating, and recording. This slowness can create a contemplative frame around the question. The method encourages attention before interpretation begins.

This quality links the method to `oracle-traditions`, where the process of consultation is often as meaningful as the final sign.

# Line Values

Like other I Ching methods, the yarrow stalk method generates line values. These values may represent stable Yin, stable Yang, changing Yin, or changing Yang. Six generated lines create a hexagram from bottom to top.

The method therefore connects directly to `hexagrams` and `changing-lines`. A reading may produce both an initial hexagram and a transformed hexagram if moving lines are present.

# Trigrams and Symbolic Structure

The six-line figure can also be read as two `trigrams`. The lower and upper trigram provide symbolic images and relationships. This connects the method to `eight-trigrams-divination`, `eight-trigrams`, and `bagua`.

The yarrow stalk method does not only produce numbers. It produces a structure that belongs to a wider symbolic world.

# Comparison with Coin Casting

Coin casting is faster and more common in many modern contexts. The yarrow stalk method is more elaborate and traditional. Both are valid cultural methods within I Ching practice, but they create different experiences of consultation.
