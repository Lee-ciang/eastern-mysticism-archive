# SEO Decision Rules

**Project:** Eastern Mysticism Archive  
**Status:** Permanent operating document  
**Current KAPF stage:** Performance Validation

## Purpose

These rules govern SEO prioritization during Performance Validation and future growth. They ensure that publishing and optimization decisions follow observed demand, project quality standards, and the Knowledge Asset Production Framework rather than intuition alone.

## 1. Search Console Data Overrides Assumptions

Google Search Console is the primary evidence source for determining which pages, entities, queries, and clusters Google is testing.

Editorial judgment remains necessary for interpretation, but it must not override clear performance evidence without a documented reason. Decisions should consider query relevance, impressions, clicks, average position, click-through rate, time range, and temporary noise.

## 2. Strengthen Successful Clusters Before Expanding New Topics

Never expand a new topic before strengthening an existing cluster that already shows relevant search demand.

When a cluster receives meaningful impressions, priority goes to:

- Improving its authority page.
- Strengthening supporting pages.
- Closing semantic gaps.
- Improving contextual internal links.
- Adding useful diagrams, comparisons, and FAQs.
- Resolving overlap or weak navigation.

## 3. Optimize Tested Pages Before Publishing New Content

Pages already receiving relevant Google impressions should be optimized before unrelated new pages are published.

An impression indicates that Google is evaluating a page for a query. The first response should improve usefulness, intent alignment, authority, presentation, and cluster support. Creating more pages without strengthening tested assets can spread editorial effort too thin.

## 4. Follow the KAPF Roadmap

SEO work must follow this sequence:

```text
Knowledge
    ↓
Authority
    ↓
Performance
    ↓
Flywheel
```

### Knowledge

Establish accurate entities, useful coverage, stable structure, and a coherent knowledge graph.

### Authority

Develop cornerstone pages, strong supporting clusters, reliable internal links, and reusable visual knowledge assets.

### Performance

Use Search Console and site data to optimize pages demonstrating real opportunity.

### Flywheel

Reuse proven knowledge assets across search, visual discovery, distribution, products, and future formats while feeding evidence back into the system.

**Current stage: Performance Validation**

The current objective is to validate which established knowledge assets Google understands and rewards. Broad content expansion is not the default activity.

## 5. Traffic Quality Is More Important Than Content Quantity

Relevant impressions, qualified clicks, useful engagement, and clear intent alignment matter more than page count.

A smaller number of authoritative pages serving the correct audience is more valuable than a large inventory of weak, overlapping, or disconnected content. Production metrics must never substitute for user value.

## 6. Optimize the Cluster, Not Only the Page

Every optimization decision should consider:

- Supporting entities.
- Inbound contextual links.
- Outbound explanatory links.
- Competing or overlapping pages.
- Missing bridge concepts.
- Related diagrams and comparisons.

A page can underperform because its cluster is incomplete even when its own article is strong.

## 7. Preserve Search Intent Boundaries

Do not merge or split pages solely because keywords look similar. Determine whether users expect a definition, symbolic explanation, historical overview, comparison, cultural interpretation, or specific entity page.

Distinct intent should remain distinct when each page provides unique value. Overlap should be resolved only after reviewing query data and content scope.

## 8. Improve Existing Assets Before Adding Assets

Review depth, clarity, FAQs, visuals, links, headings, comparisons, accessibility, and semantic completeness before proposing a new page.

New content is justified when a meaningful user need cannot be answered responsibly within the current information architecture.

## 9. Use Data Over Sufficient Time

Avoid major decisions based on isolated daily changes. Use a comparison period appropriate to traffic volume and indexing maturity, noting seasonality, recent publishing, major site changes, and branded versus non-branded queries.

## 10. Maintain Technical and Editorial Quality

SEO work must not introduce:

- Duplicate slugs.
- Broken related or body links.
- Orphan pages.
- Unsupported claims.
- Keyword stuffing.
- Repetitive filler.
- Inaccurate diagrams.
- Accessibility regressions.
- Unnecessary architectural changes.

## Decision Sequence

1. Review Search Console evidence.
2. Confirm query and audience intent.
3. Identify the relevant page and cluster.
4. Diagnose content, link, visual, or technical gaps.
5. Choose the smallest high-value optimization.
6. Define the expected outcome and guardrails.
7. Implement and validate.
8. Record the change date.
9. Monitor results over an appropriate period.
10. Feed findings into the next decision.

## Governing Principle

Data identifies where attention is deserved. Editorial judgment determines how to improve the knowledge asset. KAPF ensures that the improvement strengthens the whole system.
