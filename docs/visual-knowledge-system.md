# Eastern Mysticism Archive Visual Knowledge System

## 1. Purpose

The Visual Knowledge System defines the permanent image standard for Eastern Mysticism Archive. Its purpose is to make complex traditions easier to understand through consistent, educational, and reusable visuals.

Images should support learning rather than decorate pages. Each visual should clarify a concept, relationship, sequence, symbol, ritual context, historical development, or comparison that would otherwise require long explanation.

The system supports:

- Educational value through clear diagrams, maps, timelines, and symbolic illustrations.
- User understanding by making abstract concepts such as Dao, Qi, Yin Yang, Bagua, Five Elements, Feng Shui, rituals, deities, and folk beliefs visually legible.
- SEO support through descriptive file names, alt text, contextual placement, and reusable visual assets.
- Google Images visibility through high-quality, topic-specific, non-generic images.
- Pinterest discovery through vertical educational graphics and visually consistent article assets.
- Visual consistency across hundreds of archive pages.
- Long-term reusable assets that can support future clusters without redesigning the image style from scratch.

Every image should feel like part of the same archive: scholarly, calm, symbolic, culturally respectful, and designed for readers who want to learn.

## 2. Overall Brand Style

The approved primary visual identity combines:

Ancient Chinese manuscript style
- Aged paper textures.
- Ink wash influence.
- Hand-drawn diagram feeling.
- Classical scroll, chart, and archive references.
- Subtle weathering without making text hard to read.

Modern educational infographic
- Clear hierarchy.
- Clean labels.
- Structured diagrams.
- Balanced spacing.
- Search-friendly, readable layouts.

The atmosphere should be contemplative, historical, and precise. Visuals should feel like museum-quality educational plates: refined, quiet, and informative rather than mystical spectacle.

The tone should be:

- Scholarly but approachable.
- Cultural and historical rather than sensational.
- Symbolic without making supernatural claims.
- Warm and human, with strong readability.

Consistency principles:

- Use the same palette family across all images.
- Keep label systems predictable.
- Use recurring borders, arrows, dividers, and annotation styles.
- Avoid photorealistic people as the default style.
- Avoid neon colors, fantasy lighting, glossy 3D effects, and generic stock-photo aesthetics.
- Prefer diagrams, symbolic illustration, manuscript plates, maps, and educational layouts.

## 3. Color System

Recommended palette:

| Role | HEX | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Background | `#F3E7D0` | Aged manuscript paper, diagram fields, scroll-style backgrounds. |
| Ink | `#2B241C` | Primary text, line art, diagram outlines, icon strokes. |
| Accent Red | `#A33A2A` | Seals, key highlights, directional markers, important symbols. |
| Gold | `#C49A45` | Sacred emphasis, hierarchy markers, subtle borders, imperial or celestial references. |
| Muted Green | `#6F7F59` | Wood phase, nature, growth, mountains, landscape notes. |
| Muted Blue | `#4E6F82` | Water, heavens, rivers, night, cosmology, directional contrast. |

Usage rules:

- Background should dominate most educational images.
- Ink should remain the main readability color.
- Accent Red should be used sparingly for focal points, seals, selected nodes, or warnings.
- Gold should mark authority, center points, celestial order, or refined decorative rules.
- Muted Green should support nature, wood, mountains, ritual plants, and seasonal growth.
- Muted Blue should support water, sky, north, night, flow, and cosmological depth.

Avoid one-note palettes. Every image should include enough contrast to remain readable on mobile screens.

## 4. Typography

Typography should prioritize readability over ornament.

Titles:

- Large, high-contrast, and short.
- Use a refined serif or readable display serif when available.
- Avoid overly decorative calligraphy for English text.

Subtitles:

- Smaller than titles, but still clearly separated.
- Use concise explanatory phrasing.
- Support the visual, not repeat the article title mechanically.

Labels:

- Use simple sans-serif or highly readable serif.
- Keep labels short.
- Place labels close to the relevant symbol, node, line, or region.
- Maintain consistent label size within the same image.

Body text:

- Use sparingly.
- Prefer short annotation blocks of one to two lines.
- Never place long paragraphs inside images.
- Ensure body text remains readable at thumbnail size where possible.

Chinese characters may be used as symbolic accents, but English readability must remain primary for the archive audience.

## 5. Image Categories

### Hero Illustration

Purpose: Establish the main visual identity of an article or hub page.

Recommended dimensions: `1600 x 900`.

Typical usage: Hub pages, major authority pages, cluster introductions, Open Graph derivatives.

### Knowledge Diagram

Purpose: Explain the structure of a concept, symbol, system, or doctrine.

Recommended dimensions: `1400 x 1000`.

Typical usage: Bagua, Five Elements, Yin Yang, Qi, Lo Shu, Taiji, cosmology pages.

### Relationship Map

Purpose: Show links among entities, concepts, rituals, symbols, or clusters.

Recommended dimensions: `1600 x 1200`.

Typical usage: Topic cluster hubs, deity networks, ritual systems, zodiac relationships.

### Timeline

Purpose: Show historical development or seasonal sequence.

Recommended dimensions: `1800 x 900`.

Typical usage: Daoist history, festival cycle, calendar systems, I Ching transmission.

### Comparison Diagram

Purpose: Clarify similarities and differences between related concepts.

Recommended dimensions: `1400 x 1000`.

Typical usage: Yin and Yang, Five Elements cycles, Bagua arrangements, talisman types.

### Symbol Illustration

Purpose: Present a single symbol with annotations and cultural explanation.

Recommended dimensions: `1200 x 1200`.

Typical usage: Dragon symbol, Taiji diagram, Lo Shu square, talisman seals, sacred animals.

### Flow Diagram

Purpose: Explain process, ritual sequence, circulation, transformation, or conceptual movement.

Recommended dimensions: `1400 x 1000`.

Typical usage: Qi circulation, talisman burning, offering sequence, Five Elements cycles.

### Map

Purpose: Represent spatial, directional, cosmological, temple, or regional relationships.

Recommended dimensions: `1600 x 1200`.

Typical usage: Feng Shui layouts, sacred mountains, cosmological directions, temple space.

### Icon Set

Purpose: Provide reusable small visual units for categories, concepts, phases, animals, rituals, and symbols.

Recommended dimensions: `512 x 512` per icon.

Typical usage: Navigation aids, cluster summaries, cards, diagrams, internal visual systems.

## 7. Visual Layout Rules

Spacing:

- Use generous outer margins.
- Avoid crowding labels against edges.
- Leave enough negative space for mobile readability.
- Keep diagrams balanced around a visible center or clear reading path.

Margins:

- Minimum safe margin: 6 percent of image width.
- For Open Graph images, keep critical text away from all edges.
- For Pinterest graphics, reserve top and bottom breathing room.

Icon usage:

- Use simple ink-style icons.
- Keep stroke weight consistent.
- Avoid mixing flat icons, 3D icons, and painterly icons in the same asset.
- Use icons as teaching aids, not decoration.

Arrow styles:

- Prefer thin ink arrows with modest arrowheads.
- Use curved arrows for cycles.
- Use straight arrows for sequence or causation.
- Use dotted arrows for symbolic association or softer relationships.

Borders:

- Use subtle manuscript-style borders.
- Avoid heavy frames that compete with the content.
- Gold or ink borders may mark formal diagrams.

Labels:

- Keep label text concise.
- Use high contrast.
- Align labels consistently.
- Use leader lines only when direct placement would clutter the diagram.

Hierarchy:

- One dominant focal point per image.
- Secondary nodes should be visually smaller or lighter.
- Accent Red and Gold should guide attention, not overwhelm the composition.

## 8. AI Image Prompt Standard

Reusable master prompt:

```text
Create a museum-quality educational illustration for Eastern Mysticism Archive in an ancient Chinese manuscript style combined with a modern educational infographic layout. Use aged parchment paper, refined black ink linework, subtle ink wash texture, muted red seal accents, soft gold highlights, muted green and muted blue supporting tones. The image should explain the topic clearly with balanced composition, readable English labels, clean diagram hierarchy, generous margins, and a calm scholarly atmosphere. Avoid photorealistic people, fantasy spectacle, neon colors, glossy 3D effects, clutter, watermark, logo, fake signatures, and illegible text. High resolution, professional, culturally respectful, educational, suitable for Google Images, Pinterest, and long-term archive reuse.
```

Prompt adaptation rules:

- Replace "the topic" with the exact page topic.
- Add the required diagram type: hero illustration, relationship map, timeline, comparison diagram, flow diagram, map, symbol illustration, or icon set.
- Specify dimensions when generating.
- Keep labels short and verify them manually.
- Never rely on AI-generated text without review.

## 9. File Naming Convention

Examples:

- `bagua-hero.webp`
- `bagua-diagram-elements.webp`
- `bagua-relationship-map.webp`
- `dao-timeline.webp`
- `five-elements-comparison.webp`

Naming rules:

- Use lowercase.
- Use hyphens, not underscores.
- Begin with the page slug or cluster slug.
- Add the image function after the slug.
- Use descriptive but concise names.
- Use `.webp` for final web assets unless another format is required.
- Avoid generic names such as `image1.webp`, `diagram-final.webp`, or `new-hero.webp`.

Recommended pattern:

```text
{slug}-{image-type}.webp
{slug}-{image-type}-{specific-topic}.webp
{cluster-slug}-{relationship-map}.webp
```

## 10. Folder Structure

Recommended complete image directory:

```text
public/images/
  symbols/
  feng-shui/
  taoism/
  rituals/
  folk-beliefs/
  diagrams/
  maps/
  timelines/
  icons/
```

Usage guidelines:

- Place page-specific hero and symbol images in the matching category folder.
- Place cross-category diagrams in `diagrams/`.
- Place geographic, cosmological, temple, or directional maps in `maps/`.
- Place historical or seasonal sequence graphics in `timelines/`.
- Place reusable category icons and concept icons in `icons/`.

If an image belongs equally to multiple categories, store it by its primary page context and reference it consistently.

## 11. Accessibility

ALT text principles:

- Describe the educational content of the image.
- Include the main concept, symbol, or relationship shown.
- Keep alt text concise but meaningful.
- Avoid keyword stuffing.
- Do not begin with "image of" unless needed for clarity.

Good ALT text examples:

- `Annotated Bagua diagram showing eight trigrams arranged around a central Taiji symbol.`
- `Lo Shu Square diagram with numbers one through nine arranged in a three-by-three grid.`
- `Timeline showing major stages in the development of Taoist cosmology.`

Accessibility guidelines:

- Maintain strong contrast between text and background.
- Do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning.
- Pair color coding with labels or icons.
- Keep text readable on mobile.
- Avoid placing important text over complex texture.

SEO image guidelines:

- Use descriptive file names.
- Use accurate alt text.
- Place images near relevant article sections.
- Add captions when they improve understanding.
- Prefer original educational visuals over generic stock images.
- Reuse consistent visual assets to strengthen topical authority.

## 12. Future Expansion

The Visual Knowledge System is designed to scale from 200 pages to 1000+ pages without losing identity.

At 200 pages, the priority is to create core hub visuals for major authority pages, including Dao, Qi, Yin Yang, Bagua, Five Elements, Feng Shui, Taoist Cosmology, ritual systems, festivals, deities, talismans, and symbolic clusters.

At 500 pages, the archive should add reusable diagrams for recurring systems:

- Yin-Yang relationships.
- Five Elements cycles.
- Bagua arrangements.
- Qi flow diagrams.
- Ritual sequence diagrams.
- Deity relationship maps.
- Festival and calendar timelines.
- Symbol and animal illustration sets.

At 1000+ pages, the system should rely on templates:

- Standard hero template.
- Standard diagram template.
- Standard relationship map template.
- Standard timeline template.
- Standard symbol plate template.
- Standard Pinterest template.
- Standard Open Graph template.

This allows new clusters to receive visuals quickly while preserving archive identity. The goal is not to make every page visually identical. The goal is to make every image feel like it belongs to the same educational world: manuscript-based, diagram-friendly, culturally respectful, readable, and built for long-term knowledge discovery.
