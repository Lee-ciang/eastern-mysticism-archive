# Eastern Mysticism Archive Taxonomy Blueprint

## Purpose

This document defines a scalable archive taxonomy and topic relationship blueprint for Eastern Mysticism Archive. The goal is to support encyclopedia-style growth across Chinese mysticism, Taoism, folk religion, Feng Shui, ritual systems, symbolism, cosmology, divination, and related East Asian esoteric traditions.

The archive should grow as a structured research reference rather than a loose blog. Categories should support topical authority, internal linking, future SEO expansion, and eventual relationship graphing between symbols, practices, beliefs, objects, entities, places, and ritual systems.

## Taxonomy Principles

- Keep primary categories broad enough to scale into hundreds of entries.
- Use secondary subdomains to organize topical clusters without creating too many top-level routes.
- Avoid adding new public categories until enough entries exist to justify them.
- Preserve cultural, historical, and educational framing.
- Link entries by meaning, function, tradition, object, ritual context, and cosmological system.
- Treat controversial, supernatural, or medical-adjacent topics carefully with clear informational framing.

## Cross-Category Relationship Concepts

The archive should model relationships explicitly. Each entry can eventually support relationship fields beyond simple related links.

### Relationship Types

- **Symbolizes:** A symbol represents a concept, entity, force, or cosmological principle.
- **Used In:** An object, talisman, chant, or symbol appears in a ritual or practice.
- **Derived From:** A concept originates from a text, doctrine, myth, or older tradition.
- **Associated With:** A broad cultural or traditional association.
- **Protects Against:** A protective practice or talisman addresses a spirit, misfortune, taboo, or harmful influence.
- **Belongs To:** A topic belongs to a tradition, school, festival, or ritual system.
- **Occurs During:** A ritual or belief is tied to a festival, season, lunar date, or calendrical period.
- **Requires Object:** A ritual uses a sacred object, talisman, incense, offering, or altar tool.
- **Explained By:** A practical topic is explained by a cosmological or doctrinal concept.
- **Represented By:** A deity, myth, or principle is represented through a symbol or object.

### Example Relationship Chains

- Bagua -> symbolizes -> trigrams -> explained by -> yin-yang -> used in -> Feng Shui
- Talisman burning -> used in -> Taoist ritual -> uses object -> fu talisman -> protects against -> harmful spirits
- Ghost Month -> occurs during -> lunar seventh month -> involves -> hungry ghosts -> requires -> offerings
- Luopan -> used in -> Feng Shui -> explained by -> directions, Five Phases, trigrams
- Door gods -> protective practice -> protects threshold -> associated with -> folk religion

## Risk and SEO Noise Assessment

### Low-Risk, High-Authority Topics

- Symbols
- Cosmology
- Taoism basics
- Feng Shui tools
- Festivals
- Sacred objects
- Historical ritual descriptions

These topics are educational, internally linkable, and less likely to trigger misleading or sensational interpretations.

### Medium-Risk Topics

- Talismans
- Protective practices
- Spirit entities
- Afterlife beliefs
- Divination

These can perform well, but content should avoid claims of guaranteed supernatural effects. Use cultural, historical, symbolic, and religious framing.

### High-Risk or Noisy SEO Topics

- Astrology predictions
- Love compatibility
- Wealth luck cures
- Exorcism claims
- Health or healing rituals
- Curse removal
- Guaranteed protection
- Spirit communication instructions

These topics may attract traffic but can dilute the archive's authority if handled like clickbait or advice content. If covered, they should be framed as cultural history, belief systems, or textual tradition rather than actionable promises.

## Editorial Guardrails

- Do not present mystical claims as verified factual outcomes.
- Avoid medical, financial, or guaranteed protection advice.
- Distinguish Taoist philosophy, religious Taoism, folk religion, and modern popular belief when relevant.
- Prefer neutral phrasing: "is believed to," "is traditionally associated with," "is used in," "is interpreted as."
- Make entries useful as cultural and historical references.
- Build internal links from foundational concepts to specialized applications.

## Summary Blueprint

The archive should grow from a stable foundation of symbols, cosmology, Taoism, folk religion, rituals, and Feng Shui. Talismans, divination, spirit entities, sacred objects, festivals, mythology, temples, afterlife beliefs, astrology, and protective practices should be developed as interconnected clusters.

The strongest long-term strategy is to treat every article as both an encyclopedia entry and a node in a relationship graph. Foundational concepts should explain applied practices, while applied practices should link back to the symbols, cosmology, entities, objects, and traditions that give them meaning.
